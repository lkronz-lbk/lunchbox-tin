Help

Questions, answered

Short answers to what parents ask. Anything else: hello@lunchsorted.app.

How does it pick the week?

From the foods on your list, matched so each box goes together: something crunchy against a soft main, protein when the main is light, something tangy to cut the salt. Each compartment shows the words the pairing used. It is not a random draw, and it never rewrites a day that has already gone.

Can I plan more than one week?

Two: this week and the one after. On Week, Next › beside the date shows the week after, with Plan it if nothing is there yet and ‹ This to come back; Shuffle all and Shuffle day both work on whichever week you are looking at. Shop lists what to buy for it under Next week, with the same checks. When this week has gone, the week after becomes this week on its own. Pack, the kid’s pick and the after-school review only ever look at this week.

How do I change one thing?

On Week, tap the compartment (the small swap arrows). Pick another food, or shuffle just that one. Tap a compartment and choose "Don’t change this one" to keep it through the next shuffle. "Shuffle day" redraws that one day; "Shuffle all" redraws the week. With more than one lunchbox either one first asks whose boxes, and every shuffle offers Undo for a few seconds.

How do I add foods to the list?

On Foods, tap Ideas and tap anything your kid will eat — that is the built-in idea bank, and it is free for good. Add your own, at the bottom of the list, writes a food in your own words with what to buy for it; that one is part of the Household plan. With more than one lunchbox, whatever you add goes into every box unless you tap a name under “Add it to” to leave one out.

Where do I find the recipes?

On the Recipes tab, along the bottom. Food lists belong to each lunchbox; recipes belong to the household, so they are one list however many kids you pack for — the ones you have kept first, then the two the app comes with, which are free along with the rest of the bank. Those two — a Mediterranean quinoa salad and a bean and avocado wrap — were written and tasted by school teams for the USDA, which is why they are the only two we ship: a recipe nobody has cooked is worse than no recipe. For any other dish in the bank, Import a recipe brings in the one you already trust. The search box matches a name or an ingredient, so typing “lemon” shows you what you could make with what is in the cupboard. You can also reach a recipe from the food it belongs to: tap the food on Foods, then Open the recipe; a food that has one carries a small Recipe tag beside its name. Cook it step by step puts one step on the screen at a time, with the ingredients a tap away and a box to tick as each one goes in; nothing you tick is saved, so it never turns up on the other parent’s phone. The same recipe is on the compartment when you tap it on Week, and named under the box on Pack, which is where you are standing the night before.

Can I see a recipe in grams instead of cups?

Open the recipe and tap Grams — or Cups, for a recipe that came in written in grams; it converts both ways. A cup of flour is weighed, water and oil are poured in millilitres, an oven set in Fahrenheit carries its Celsius, and a spoonful of salt is left as a spoonful, because six grams of salt is not a measurement anyone uses. A cup of cherry tomatoes stays a cup: we only convert what can honestly be converted. The phone remembers which measures your kitchen works in. The + and − beside the number make more or less — lunches, for the app’s own mains; whatever the recipe counted in, for anything else — and scale every amount with them.

Can I bring in a recipe from somewhere else?

Yes, two ways, both under Recipes → Import a recipe. Paste the address of a recipe page and the app reads the ingredients, the steps, the time and the yield straight off it — that works on most recipe blogs, whether they publish the recipe for search engines or just write it out under an Ingredients heading. Some pages still will not give it up, and a few sites refuse us outright; when that happens the app says so and the paste box below is the way through. That reading happens on our server, so it needs you signed in: the address goes to us, we send back the recipe and nothing else, and we keep neither the address nor the page. A video does not: there is nothing under a reel or a pin but the words its maker typed, so copy those and paste them into the box instead, and the app reads them on your phone, signed in or not, without sending anything anywhere. Either way what comes back is yours to name and keep, and it is kept for its own sake: putting it on a lunchbox’s food list is offered afterwards rather than done for you, so you can keep a recipe you have no plans to pack this week. If you do add it, the shopping line and what it contains are filled in for you to correct — check that part before you save it, because the school rules and the allergens go by it. Keeping a recipe of your own is part of the Household plan; the idea bank and the two recipes that come with it stay free to cook from.

Can I put a recipe on a food I already have, or get rid of one?

Yes. Tap the food on Foods. If it has no recipe yet it says so, with Add a recipe underneath — paste the address of a recipe page, or paste the recipe itself, exactly as above. It is kept on the Recipes tab like any other, and the food points at it — as does the same dish in your other lunchboxes, because one recipe serves them all. To get rid of one, open it from the Recipes tab and remove it there; there is an Undo, the foods that used it stay on their lists, and a dish named after one of the two the app comes with falls back to that built-in recipe. Like importing, this is part of the Household plan, because it writes to your household.

Why did a food stop showing up?

A school rule or an allergen you set in the lunchbox settings — the sliders at the top beside the ?, or Account → Lunchboxes — is keeping it out. The Foods tab marks each food it skips and says which rule. Change the rule, or remove the food. Rules never delete a food, and they never rewrite a day that has already gone.

Can I put in a food a rule says no to?

Yes. On Week, tap the compartment, then tap the food — it reads “has gluten — tap to use it anyway”. It goes in, the toast names the rule with an Undo, and it stays flagged: a ! on that compartment and an Against the rules mark on the day, on Week and on Pack. That is the whole of it. The flag lasts as long as that food is in that compartment; shuffle it or pick something else and it is gone. The rule still applies to every draw, and if the kid’s pick trades that box to another day, the flag travels with it.

I have more than one kid. Do I set it all up twice?

No. Add a lunchbox per kid; each keeps its own rules, food list and week, because two kids can be at two schools. Then:

One plan, matched. Plan the week draws them together. The fullest food list leads, and every other box starts from the same foods, swapping only where that box’s rules or its own list say otherwise — so four lunches are the same four lunches, bar the kid who can’t have wheat. Shuffle all on Week is that draw whenever matching is on; with more than one lunchbox it first asks whose boxes, all checked, and the boxes you leave out keep what they have. Every shuffle offers Undo for a few seconds. Boxes can only match on foods both lists have; add a food to every box from Foods to match more. Turn it off under Account → Lunchboxes → Match the boxes and each box is drawn on its own.

One food, every box. When you add a food, from the idea bank or your own, it goes into every lunchbox unless you say otherwise. Tap a name under “Add it to” to leave a box out; that is where the one weird food only one of them eats goes. A box whose rules keep the food out is skipped, and the app says which.

Tabs across them. Pack, Week, Foods and the lunchbox settings each show one lunchbox at a time, with the names as tabs along the top; one tap each. On Pack each tab carries a check once that box is packed, or a small amber ring while an answer about that box is still owed, and one line says who is still to pack. Shop is one list for the household and never splits.

If I change one kid’s Tuesday, do the others change?

No. Shuffling one lunchbox, or swapping one compartment, changes that lunchbox only. Once you have changed a box on purpose, nothing drags the others along. To match them all up again, tap Shuffle all on Week and leave every box checked.

Where does the shopping list come from?

Every planned box, as what to buy: a turkey and cheese pinwheel goes on the list as deli turkey, cheese slices and tortillas, grouped by aisle, with a count when two dishes share a thing. Check off what the pantry already has. Copy puts it on the clipboard; on a phone, the share button sends it to Notes, Reminders or a text.

How does packing work?

Pack shows the next school day’s box with a flag when it needs an ice pack, a sealed container, or is missing protein. One check marks the box packed. With more than one kid it shows one box at a time, a tab for each along the top, and a line underneath says how many are ready and names who is still to pack; tap the name to go to that box. If you pack the night before, say so under Account → Lunchboxes and from three o’clock Pack shows tomorrow’s box, the one you are packing. From three o’clock, when the box is home, it asks what came home — in that box’s tab, with a ring on any other tab still owed — or tap Answer later and a one-line reminder with Answer now takes its place until you do, until the next three o’clock, or the next time you open the app. A red dot on the Pack tab means an answer is owed, and the iPhone app can remind you at three (switch it on under Account → Lunchboxes). From three, today’s box is done: nothing re-draws it, and the shopping list drops it. What you say about a box is remembered against the food, and the next week leans toward what actually gets eaten. A food that comes home twice in a row is rested for three weeks, even when the other boxes are having it.

What is kid’s pick?

In the lunchbox settings (the sliders at the top, or Account → Lunchboxes), switch on "They pick their box each day". The night before, hand them the phone: part by part, two pictures at a time, or the whole box against the next one. Either way the choice comes from the week you already shopped for, so nothing new to buy. Add a photo of the real thing beside a food for a child who cannot read yet. The iPhone app can remind you at the time you choose.

Do I need an account?

No. Everything stays on your phone until you sign in. Sign in with your email, no password, to share the week with the other parent, give a caretaker the week and the pack list, or keep a backup. If someone invited you in, Leave this household under Account → Account takes you back to a phone of your own and leaves the lunches with the household. Delete my account and data is on that same page for everyone: it ends your sign-in and this email address, and if you are the one who set the household up it takes the household off our servers and off this phone and ends everyone else’s sign-in to it. It asks you to type DELETE first, because it cannot be undone. What is already on someone else’s phone stays on their phone, so copy your lunches out first if you want a record.

How does signing in work? There’s no password.

You give your email and we send two things: a link and a short code. Tap the link and a page opens with one button that signs you in. If you keep the planner on your home screen, the link opens in Safari rather than in it — so sign that one in with the code instead: open the planner from your home screen, go to the Account tab, and type the code in. Either one works once, on its own, for fifteen minutes. Ask for a new one any time from the Account tab.

How do I clear the lunches off a phone?

If you have never signed in, Erase everything under Account → This phone wipes the lot — two taps, and no copy left. Once you are signed in it disappears, because the lunches are the household’s rather than that one phone’s, and wiping them there would empty the other parent’s phone too. Instead Sign out leaves them behind and takes nothing with you, and Delete my account and data ends the account itself. Clear the plans is the gentle one: it empties the weeks and keeps your foods, rules and lunchboxes.

What is free, and what does the plan cost?

Planning the week, the shopping list and the pack list are free for good, for one lunchbox, and the built-in idea bank is free to build that list from, with its two recipes free to cook from. Foods written in your own words, recipes of your own — pasted in or read off a page — kid’s pick, the after-school review, a pantry that remembers, more lunchboxes and sharing are the Household plan. Every new household gets the whole plan for three weeks, no card. Prices are on the front page; refunds within 14 days, no questions.

Why can’t I add my own food any more?

Your three weeks are up. Nothing was removed: every food you added is still on the list, still gets planned, and still goes on the shopping list — recipes and all. Adding another one in your own words, or keeping a recipe of your own, is part of the Household plan. The idea bank stays free, so the list can still grow, and the recipes that come with it stay free to cook from.

Where do I see what I’m paying, and how do I stop it?

Account tab → Subscription. It shows the plan you are on, what it costs, and the date it renews or ends — or that you paid once and it never renews. Manage billing opens Stripe for the card, the receipts and cancelling; it opens for whoever owns the household and whoever paid. Cancel and the plan keeps working until the date shown, then one lunchbox, the week, the shopping list, the pack list and the idea bank stay free. Every lunchbox, food, check and answer you added stays where it is.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Once opened, the planner works without a network. Changes sync when you are back online if you are signed in.

Something is wrong. Where do I say so?

Tap the ? at the top of any tab, or the Contact support row on the Account tab — both open the same answers. Something is wrong and Ask a question open an email with the build and the phone already filled in, and Send feedback is a short form. Or write to hello@lunchsorted.app.
