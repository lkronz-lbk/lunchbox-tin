Privacy

Short version: signed out, the planner keeps everything on your device. Signed in, your household's lunches are also stored on our servers so a second phone can share them, and the only things we hold are your email address and that household. Like every website, the company that hosts it keeps ordinary server logs (the address your connection comes from, your browser type) for a short time.

The planner

Everything you enter — lunchbox names, food lists, recipes you keep, school rules, plans, shopping checkmarks — is stored in your own browser's local storage on the device you used. Until you sign in, it is never sent anywhere. That is also why reading a recipe off a web page asks you to sign in first: that one feature sends the address you paste to our server, which reads the page and sends you back the recipe. We keep neither the address nor the page. Pasting a recipe in as text is read on your phone and sends nothing, signed in or not. Clearing your browser data or deleting the installed app deletes it too. The planner's Account tab, under Account, has a copy-out button so you can keep your own backup or move to another phone.

Signing in

You can sign in with an email address so the same household works on more than one phone. There is no password: we email you a link that works once. Signed in, a copy of your household — the same data described above — is stored on our servers (hosted by Netlify, database by Neon, both in the United States) and shared only with the people you invite into it. We keep your email address, the household, the sign-in sessions for your phones, the day you last used the app and a count of the days you have used it; nothing else. That count is a single number: we do not keep a record of which days, or of anything you did inside the app. To limit sign-in abuse we keep a hashed form of the connection address a request came from, for one day. Signing out clears that phone; the household stays on our servers, and signing in again brings it back. Leaving a household leaves the lunches with it. Delete my account and data, under Account → Account and confirmed by typing DELETE, ends your sign-in and this email address; if you set the household up it also removes the household from our servers and from that phone and cannot be undone; if someone else you invited was in it, their sign-in ends too, and whatever their phone held stays on their phone. A caretaker you invite sees only the plan and the pack list, never the school rules, allergens or anyone's email address.

Paying

If you buy the Household plan, payment is taken by Stripe on Stripe's own page. Stripe receives your card details, your email address and, for tax, your billing address; we receive only a customer reference, the plan you bought and whether it is paid. We never see or store your card. Stripe's own privacy policy covers what it holds. The plan is recorded against your household, not against a person, so everyone you invite has it too.

The forms

If you send the feedback form, what you typed and the email you gave are stored by Netlify, which hosts this site, and read by me; they are used to fix and improve the app and for nothing else. If you submit the form on the home page, your email address is stored by Netlify, so I can email you when the features mentioned there are ready. It is not sold, shared, or used for anything else, and every email includes a way to unsubscribe. Ask at the address below and I'll delete it.

Analytics and tracking

The front page of this site (not the planner) uses Google Analytics to count visits: which pages are read and roughly where from, with IP addresses shortened. It sets Google’s analytics cookies for that. The planner at /app/ loads no analytics, no advertising pixels and no third-party cookies, ever. Fonts are loaded from Google Fonts, which means Google receives the request for the font files; nothing else about you is sent with it. If you open the planner inside the Claude app rather than in a browser, a “Save a backup file” button appears under Account → Account; it hands your lunches to that app’s own save dialog, and nowhere else.

Children's information

The planner is set up and run by an adult. The one part a child touches is the “kid’s pick” screen, which shows the week's lunchboxes and never asks them for anything. A photo you add to a food is stored with the rest of the household, shrunk to a thumbnail, and shown only inside the app. A child's name and any allergens you enter are personal information about a minor: they stay on your device unless you sign in, in which case they are stored with the rest of the household as described above, never shared beyond the people in it, and never used for anything else. You are welcome to use initials or a nickname instead — the app never needs a real name to work.
