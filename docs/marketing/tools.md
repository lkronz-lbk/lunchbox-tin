# The tools — what to use, what to skip, and why

The question that started this: *"there are a ton of apps like Mako or Munch
that claim to turn my website into marketing content — which are the best?"*

The honest answer is that the category is real, the tools work, and **almost
none of them are built for us.** They exist to let a performance marketer test
forty ad variations a week against a pixel. We have no pixel, no ad budget, and
a $29-a-year product. What they automate is the part we are not doing; what
they cannot do — a specific voice, a real screen recording, a parent's actual
sentence — is the part that will work.

Prices are as found in September 2026 and move constantly. Verify before
paying; treat every per-video cost as an estimate.

---

## The four categories

### 1. Website → social posts

**What they do:** paste a URL, get captions, post ideas and a schedule.
SocialBee's Copilot builds a whole content plan from a site; Hootsuite's
OwlyWriter and Buffer's AI Assistant generate posts from a link.

**Verdict: skip.** Liz has Claude and a documented brand voice. These tools
produce competent, anonymous copy, and anonymous copy is the one thing this
product cannot afford — the whole positioning is a real parent talking to
another parent. The scheduling is worth something; the writing is not.

### 2. Website → ad creative and AI UGC video

**Mako** ("AI ad creative OS"): researches customer signals, maps creative to
awareness stages, renders on-brand assets, publishes ads to Meta and TikTok.
Reviews are genuinely good. **Creatify:** free tier, $39/mo Starter, $99/mo
Pro, roughly $3.90 per 30-second video; strongest when a physical product is in
frame. **Arcads:** ~$29 Lite, $77–110/mo with talking actors, ~$11 a video;
the most convincing AI actors, aimed at apps and SaaS. **Icon:** around $999/mo
for human-filmed UGC.

**Verdict: not now, and possibly never.** Every one of these is priced and
designed for paid acquisition at volume. Their output is ad creative, and we
are not running ads — no pixel means no optimisation loop, and without the loop
the volume is pointless. There is a second reason to be careful: an AI actor
telling parents how much she loves a lunch planner, for a product whose entire
promise is honesty about a real family's mornings, is a brand risk that no
click-through rate makes back.

**When to revisit:** once the cost of a paying household is known and paid
social is worth trying, Creatify is the sensible first test — the free tier
costs nothing and the price per video is the lowest of the group.

### 3. Long video → clips

**Munch:** $19/mo basic ($15 annual), $49/mo pro ($39 annual). Finds the
strongest moments in a long video, auto-captions, reformats per platform.
Reviewers rate the clipping well and the editing depth poorly. **OpusClip** is
the obvious alternative.

**Verdict: skip until there is long-form video to clip.** Munch's job is
turning a 40-minute podcast into twenty clips. We have no podcast. Twelve
screen recordings do not need AI to find the good bit; the good bit is the
whole thing.

**When to revisit:** if Liz ever records a long demo, a webinar or a podcast
appearance, $19 for a month is a fair trade — buy it for that month.

### 4. What to actually use

| Tool | Cost | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Canva Pro** *(owned)* | — | Pins, the school one-pager, App Store screenshots. The brand palette and templates already exist. |
| **Claude** *(owned)* | — | Guides, pin titles and descriptions, App Store metadata, email drafts. It has the product spec and the voice rules in `CLAUDE.md`. |
| **Pinterest's own scheduler** | free | Two weeks of pins queued in one Sunday block. Tailwind (~$15/mo) only if the free scheduler becomes the bottleneck. |
| **iPhone screen recording + CapCut** | free | The demo clips. The app is the ad. |
| **Netlify Analytics** | $9/mo | The only analytics that fits the no-scripts promise: server-side, no cookie, nothing from a device. |
| **Netlify Forms** *(in use)* | free | The waitlist. |
| **Resend** *(in use)* | free tier | The trial emails. When the waitlist needs a real broadcast, Resend's own broadcasts or Buttondown (~$9/mo) — not a full marketing suite. |

Total new spend: **about $10–30 a month.**

---

## The rule behind all of this

Every tool above either (a) makes something only Liz can make faster, or (b)
measures something without breaking the privacy promise. Nothing on the list
generates the message itself.

The tools that "turn your website into marketing content" are, in the end,
selling the removal of the part of the work that is actually the product's
advantage. For an ecommerce brand testing hooks at volume, that trade makes
money. For a solo-built app whose pitch is *a real parent built this because
her own mornings were terrible*, it is the wrong trade.

Buy the scheduler. Keep the voice.
