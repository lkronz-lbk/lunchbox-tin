# Onboarding email sequences of consumer phone-first apps, and how people read email on a phone

Researched 2026-10-24. Scope: (a) how admired consumer apps structure first-weeks education email, with captured examples; (b) the primary research on reading behavior the founder can cite for "short lines, one CTA, a screenshot, no paragraphs." Every figure carries its source and year; figures whose origin could not be confirmed are marked UNCONFIRMED.

A note on the capture sources used below. Sequenzy and the Reteno gallery are email-capture databases that sign up for apps and log what arrives; they record day sent, subject and preheader, but their public pages mostly do not reproduce body copy or CTA labels. Really Good Emails shows the rendered email as an image with a short curator note. So the day/subject data below is solid; body-copy quotes are thinner than the brief asked for, and the gaps say so.

## Key Question 1: What onboarding sequences do admired consumer apps run?

### Takeaway
The captured consumer-app sequences cluster at 5 to 8 emails across the first 5 to 14 days, front-loaded (day 0 within the hour, then day 1 or 2, then every 2 to 4 days), with each email carrying one idea and one button. Subjects are short (Duolingo's median is 23 characters) and the "teach a feature" email is a minority of sends: the consumer apps lean on nudges, personality and discounts far more than on how-to content, which is a real contrast to B2B SaaS sequences.

### Inferences
- For a three-email first-week series, the captured consumer apps suggest days 0, 2 and 5 (Calm's content emails land on days 2, 4 and 10; Headspace's on 1, 4 and 8; Duolingo is done by day 5). Day 1 is where every app sends a nudge, not a lesson.
- Consumer apps teach at most one thing per email and often repeat the same thing from a new angle (Notion's meeting notes four times in a week) rather than covering the product. The founder's three emails can each own one screen of the app.
- The strongest-performing version in the only published numbers (PostHog 3.1, 68% open, 16% CTR) was the personalized, short, single-ask one; the screenshot-heavy and the many-branch versions both scored lower.
- Subjects: Duolingo's 23-character median and Notion's one-feature subjects both fit inside the 33 to 37 characters an iPhone Gmail app shows (see Q3).

### Gaps
- Mealime, Cozi, HelloFresh, Instacart, Peloton, Canva and Noom: no captured onboarding sequence with day/subject data surfaced on Sequenzy, Reteno, Really Good Emails or Milled through search. Mealime's only hit was its support-site Getting Started guide, not email. A direct sign-up capture is the only reliable way to get these.
- Body copy, word counts and button labels for Duolingo, Notion and Calm are not on the public capture pages; only subjects, preheaders and days are.
- Whether any of these brands deep-link each email to a specific screen could not be verified from captures; Really Good Emails strips link targets.
- Growth.Design and Lenny's Newsletter case studies on Duolingo and Headspace cover in-app onboarding and push notifications, not the email sequence, and were not fetched.

## Key Question 2: What do practitioners who publish data say about length, cadence and number of CTAs?

### Takeaway
The numbers that exist support a short email with one button and a cadence that thins out after the first days; but the two most-quoted figures ("single CTA = 371% more clicks" and "button = 127% more clicks") trace back to untraceable sources, and the only large-sample CTA study (Omnisend, 229 million emails) found 2 to 3 CTAs beat one. Cite the behavior research in Q3 for "one CTA," not the 371% figure.

### Cited Findings
- UNCONFIRMED ORIGIN: "emails with a single CTA increased clicks by 371% and sales by 1,617%" appears in a 2014 WordStream post and is repeated by Campaign Monitor; when traced, no study, sample size or date could be found. — [Campaign Monitor knowledge base, Do CTAs help](https://campaignmonitor.com/resources/knowledge-base/do-ctas-help-to-improve-email-response-rates); origin critique in [Bloggers Passion, CTA statistics](https://bloggerspassion.com/call-to-action-statistics/)
- Omnisend analyzed 229 million emails across 40,283 campaigns (25 Nov to 2 Dec 2020) and found emails with 2 to 3 CTAs had the best click rates; more than three performed measurably worse. (Ecommerce promotional sends, not onboarding.) — reported in [Bloggers Passion, CTA statistics](https://bloggerspassion.com/call-to-action-statistics/)
- UNCONFIRMED ORIGIN: "Button-based CTAs improved click-through rates by 127%" is attributed by Campaign Monitor to its own research without a linked study. — [Campaign Monitor knowledge base](https://campaignmonitor.com/resources/knowledge-base/do-ctas-help-to-improve-email-response-rates)
- Appcues: 3 to 5 emails over 7 to 14 days, one goal and one action each, behavior-triggered. — [Appcues](https://www.appcues.com/blog/onboarding-users-with-email)
- PostHog's measured flows: roughly one email per week after the first days; per-version open and click rates above (Q1). — [PostHog](https://posthog.com/blog/how-we-built-email-onboarding)
- Braze (Appboy research over 2.5 years): "sending even a single push notification to new customers who haven't finished onboarding increased retention by 71% over a two-month period"; apps that paired push with an email, in-app message or news-feed card "saw retention rates rise 130%." No sample size or year given. — [Braze, Get it right from the start](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/get-it-right-from-the-start-onboarding-campaign)
- Subscription marketers' practice vs recommendation (2014 report): 37% send only one onboarding email; 7% send four, the report's recommended number. Old, but the only survey of subscription senders found. — [Subscription Insider, 2014](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/2014/07/31)
- UNVERIFIED AGENCY STUDY: a UK agency reports a 412,000-signup, 38-product cohort (2025) in which 7 email touches over 14 days gave 38.7% day-30 retention vs 24.4% for 3 touches over 7 days and 36.4% for 9 over 21; hybrid in-app plus email beat email-only by 27 points. No data, product names or third-party verification; figures are suspiciously precise. Do not cite as research. — [Visionary Marketing, customer onboarding statistics 2026](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/customer-onboarding-statistics-2026)
- Subject-line length: Mailchimp found subject lines under 50 characters more effective except for highly targeted audiences; Adestra found under-60-character subjects most opened, 60 to 70 a "dead zone," over 70 best for click-to-open. — [Wylie Communications, subject line length](https://www.wyliecomm.com/how-long-should-email-subject-lines-be/); [Upland Adestra](https://uplandsoftware.com/adestra/?p=1619)

### Inferences
- There is no published benchmark specifically for consumer-app education emails' length or CTA count; every "best-performing length" claim found is either ecommerce promotional data or an agency blog. The defensible position is the behavioral evidence (Q3) plus the observed practice of the apps themselves (Q1).
- The Omnisend 2 to 3 CTA result is from holiday promotional email where several products compete; it does not argue for three buttons in a one-lesson education email, but it does mean "one CTA" should be argued from focus and reading time, not from the 371% figure.

### Gaps
- Customer.io, Intercom, Userlist, Klaviyo, Mailchimp, Braze and Iterable: no 2023 to 2026 report found that gives onboarding-email length or CTA-count performance numbers. Their guides restate "one CTA, short, behavior-triggered" without data.
- Groove's "3 lessons from testing hundreds of onboarding emails" now redirects to Helply's marketing site; the original text with its numbers was not retrievable.
- The Litmus 2021 State of Email Engagement PDF downloaded but could not be text-extracted locally (no PDF library on the machine), so its device-share figure is missing (see Q3 for the 2019 and 2026 figures instead).

## Key Question 3: What does the reading-behavior research say, with exact figures?

### Takeaway
Three primary numbers carry the argument: Litmus measured the average time spent on an opened email falling to 8.97 seconds in 2022 (from 13.4 in 2018); NN/g measured newsletter readers spending 51 seconds per issue with only 19% reading thoroughly, and found scannability "about 50% more important for newsletters than for websites"; and NN/g's eyetracking (2006, reconfirmed 2017) shows the F-pattern "alive and well... both on desktop and on mobile," so anything on the right side or below the second paragraph is skipped. On an iPhone, Apple Mail shows about 48 subject characters and the Gmail app about 37, and Apple now accounts for roughly three quarters of tracked opens.

### Cited Findings

**Time spent on an email (Litmus)**
- "In 2018 the average was 13.4 seconds, in 2020 it was 11.8 seconds, in 2021 it was 10 seconds, and in 2022 it was 8.97 seconds." Litmus Email Analytics data as of 31 August 2022; read-time data is a 17% subset of the opens Litmus processed (12.1 billion opens in the 2022 period, about 1.5 billion a month). — [Litmus, Trends in Email Engagement (2022)](https://www.litmus.com/blog/trends-in-email-engagement); PDF: [Trends in Email Engagement 2022](https://www.litmus.com/wp-content/uploads/pdf/Trends-in-Email-Engagement2022.pdf)
- The 2021 figure independently: "The time spent reading an email has fallen 15% to just 10.0 seconds this year." — [WARC datapoint, 1 Oct 2021](https://www.warc.com/content/article/warc-curated-datapoints/audiences-spend-10-seconds-reading-an-email-down-15/en-gb/139248)
- The earlier high-water mark: "The average time that a recipient spends reading an email increased to 13.4 seconds in 2018, up from 11.1 seconds in 2016," from more than 10 billion opens; 61% read 8+ seconds, 23% skimmed, 15% glanced; 42% of opens on smartphones or tablets. Press release 27 June 2019. — [Litmus press release, read time up 21 percent (2019)](https://www.litmus.com/press/litmus-report-email-read-time-increased-by-21-percent/)
- Litmus's definitions: glance "fewer than 2 seconds," skim "2-7 seconds," read "8 or more seconds." — [Litmus, Read rates](https://www.litmus.com/blog/email-read-rates)
- The 2022 distribution, as reported second-hand from the Litmus data: 30% of emails looked at under 2 seconds, 41% for 2 to 8 seconds, 29% for more than 8 seconds (opens Aug 2021 to Aug 2022). The MarketingProfs chart page that carries this returned 403; the figures appeared in its search snippet and could not be re-read on the page. Treat as "reported by MarketingProfs from Litmus, 2023." — [MarketingProfs, time spent looking at an email (2023)](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2023/48537/how-much-time-do-people-typically-spend-looking-at-an-email)

**How people read newsletters and web text (Nielsen Norman Group)**
- Newsletter study: readers spent an average of 51 seconds on a newsletter after opening; 19% were read thoroughly; 69% were skimmed (40% skimmed most or all headings and paragraphs, 29% skimmed less than half); "scannability is about 50% more important for newsletters than for websites." Original study 2006, from NN/g's email newsletter usability series (five rounds 2002 to 2010, 199 design guidelines). — summarized with quotes in [Wylie Communications, Just 19% of newsletters get read thoroughly](https://wyliecomm.com/2022/08/skim-scan-read) and [Chief Marketer, The secret of e-zine success](https://www.chiefmarketer.com/the-secret-of-e-zine-success-make-them-easy-to-scan); NN/g's own article notes the series and says users "scan" and put "high-value content at the start of a message" because "users are less likely now to look beyond it": [NN/g, Email newsletters usability](http://www.nngroup.com/articles/e-mail-newsletters-usability)
- Mobile reading of newsletters in that series: users rated ease of reading newsletters on a mobile device "a miserable 3.3 on a 1–7 scale." — [NN/g, Email newsletters usability](http://www.nngroup.com/articles/e-mail-newsletters-usability)
- F-pattern: "Users first read in a horizontal movement, usually across the upper part of the content area... a second horizontal movement that typically covers a shorter area... Finally, users scan the content's left side in a vertical movement." Original 2006; 2017 update: "the F-shaped scanning pattern is alive and well in today's world — both on desktop and on mobile." Consequence: "users may skip important content simply because it appears on the right side of the page." Recommendations: "Include the most important points in the first two paragraphs," "Use headings and subheadings," "Bold important words and phrases," bullets and numbers, "Cut unnecessary content." — [NN/g, F-shaped pattern of reading (2017)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/)
- "People rarely read online — they're far more likely to scan than read word for word." Scannable text uses "clear, noticeable headings and subheadings," information "up front," "bulleted lists and bold text" and "plain language." Published April 2020 from eyetracking spanning 2006 to 2019. — [NN/g, How people read online (2020)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-people-read-online/)
- Animated GIFs in email (NN/g, 15 March 2020): 14 real retail emails, 55 respondents saw animated versions and 66 saw static versions. Animated emails drew 1.7 negative words and 1.3 positive on average; static drew 1.2 negative and 1.8 positive (animation raised negative words 40% and cut positive 30%). 31% picked "annoying" for animated versions vs 14% for static. Value rating 3.24 animated vs 3.69 static; trust 3.90 vs 4.34 (1 to 7 scales). "On average, people have a more positive reaction to emails without animated GIFs." Animation did work when it demonstrated a product, e.g. showing "how the company's tear-away chinos pull away." — [NN/g, Animated GIFs in email (2020)](https://nngroup.com/articles/gif-emails)

**Where email is opened (device and client)**
- Litmus Email Client Market Share, September 2026, from over 1.5 billion opens: Apple 76.56% (Apple Mail, iPhone, iPad and Mail Privacy Protection combined), Gmail 16.10%, Outlook 3.48%, Yahoo 2.35%, Google Android 0.94%. Caveat: Apple's Mail Privacy Protection "now impact[s] roughly 55-60% of all email opens," inflating Apple's line and hiding device detail; Litmus advises moving from open rates to clicks and conversions. "Mobile devices continue to be the primary reading environment for the majority of subscribers." — [Litmus, Email client market share](https://www.litmus.com/email-client-market-share)
- Before MPP distorted the picture: 42% of opens on smartphones or tablets (2018 data, 2019 release). — [Litmus press release (2019)](https://www.litmus.com/press/litmus-report-email-read-time-increased-by-21-percent/)
- UNCONFIRMED ORIGIN: the widely repeated "over 70% of emails opened on mobile" has no traceable Litmus source; it appears on aggregator blogs only. — [ExpertBeacon aggregator](https://expertbeacon.com/the-state-of-email-client-market-share-in-2024-what-you-need-to-know/)

**Subject line and preheader truncation on phones (measured, 2023)**
- Test with a pangram on real devices: iPhone 14 Apple Mail shows 48 subject characters and 99 preheader characters; iPhone 14 Gmail app 37 and 39; iPad 10th gen Apple Mail 39 and 75; Pixel 7 Gmail 33 and 37; Samsung S22 Ultra Gmail 36 and 40; Gmail desktop at 1400px about 88; Outlook desktop about 51. "If you want your full subject line to be visible on all major Apple and Android devices, you can only use 33 characters"; preheader "maximum... would be 37 characters" across all. — [EmailToolTester, Email subject line character limit (2023)](https://www.emailtooltester.com/en/blog/email-subject-lines-character-limit/)
- Apple Mail shows the preheader as a two-line preview under the subject ("on several lines"), unlike Gmail which runs it inline after the subject. — [Cakemail, The preheader](https://www.cakemail.ca/blog/post/the-preheader-a-forgotten-element-to-optimize-your-open-rate); Constant Contact describes the same inbox behavior: [Constant Contact, The email preheader](https://knowledgebase.constantcontact.com/articles/KnowledgeBase/7123-the-email-preheader)
- Apple publishes no character count for Mail's list view; the figures above are third-party measurements and vary with font size, device width and the number of preview lines the user sets (Settings, Mail, Preview: 0 to 5 lines). No primary Apple source found.

**Mobile email layout rules repeated by the design guides (no single primary study)**
- Single column at about 600px; images exported at 2x (a 600px email's images at 1200px) for retina phones; buttons at least 44px tall with 16px padding, following Apple's 44x44pt tap-target guideline. — [Mailsoftly, email design best practices](https://mailsoftly.com/blog/email-design-best-practices/); [Bluleadz, retina email images](https://www.bluleadz.com/blog/-how-to-retina-email-images); [Adobe, How to design your emails (PDF)](https://business.adobe.com/content/dam/dx/us/en/resources/ebooks/how-to-design-your-emails/how-to-design-your-emails.pdf)

### Inferences
- Nine seconds is enough to read a subject, one heading, one image and one button, and nothing else; several paragraphs plus numbered steps cannot be read in that window, so the current emails are being skimmed at best, which NN/g's 69%-skimmed and F-pattern findings predict.
- The F-pattern's "first two paragraphs" rule, applied to an email that gets 9 seconds, becomes "first two lines": the feature and the reason to tap it must be the first thing under the header.
- A subject of 33 characters or fewer survives every phone client measured; 48 survives Apple Mail only. With Apple at roughly three quarters of opens, a 40 to 45 character subject is a reasonable ceiling for this audience, with the first 33 characters carrying the meaning.
- Apple Mail's two-line preview makes the preheader almost a second subject line on iPhone (99 characters visible); it should say the one thing the email teaches, not "View in browser."
- NN/g's GIF result cautions against decorative animation; the exception it allows, animation that demonstrates the product, is exactly a short screen recording of one gesture in the app. A static cropped screenshot is the safer default.

### Gaps
- The 2022 glance/skim/read split (30/41/29) could not be re-read on its page (403); it is consistent with the 2019 primary figures' trend but is reported here second-hand.
- No primary research found on reading time specifically for app onboarding or educational email; Litmus's numbers are across all commercial email.
- No Apple documentation of Mail's subject or preview character limits exists; the figures are device tests.
- NN/g's full newsletter report (paid, 199 guidelines) was not accessed; the 51-second and 19% figures are quoted from coverage of NN/g's 2006 release, not from the report itself.

## Key Question 4: Is there evidence that educational tip emails drive return visits rather than deletion?

### Takeaway
Direct evidence that "tip" emails specifically bring people back into a consumer app is thin and mostly vendor-authored; what exists says onboarding messaging as a whole lifts retention (Braze/Appboy: +71% from one push, +130% when paired with email or in-app over two months), that the biggest published gain came from personalizing the email rather than adding content (PostHog 3.0 to 3.1: open 56% to 68%, CTR 7.4% to 16%), and that NN/g's reaction data shows readers judge an email as valuable or annoying in seconds, so a long tip email risks the "annoying" verdict before the tip is reached.

### Cited Findings
- Braze/Appboy: one push to users who had not finished onboarding raised two-month retention 71%; pairing push with email, in-app or news-feed card raised it 130%. Research period 2.5 years; no sample size or year. — [Braze, Get it right from the start](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/get-it-right-from-the-start-onboarding-campaign)
- PostHog: the personalized rewrite of the same sequence lifted opens from 56% to 68% and CTR from 7.4% to 16%; later versions with more emails and more branches fell to 2.4 to 4% CTR; the version with the team's own illustrations beat the one with product screenshots. — [PostHog](https://posthog.com/blog/how-we-built-email-onboarding)
- Hinge saw click-through rates rise 200% on Braze-powered campaigns; HBO Max saw a 3,000% higher subscription rate from segmented onboarding vs one-size-fits-all (vendor case studies, no methodology). — reported in [Braze customers, onboarding](https://www.braze.com/customers/onboarding) via search summary; treat as marketing claims.
- NN/g: readers rated animated marketing emails lower on value (3.24 vs 3.69) and trust (3.90 vs 4.34), and 31% called them annoying; the judgment formed on a single viewing. — [NN/g, Animated GIFs in email (2020)](https://nngroup.com/articles/gif-emails)
- Notion's captured sequence shows what a retention-minded consumer-adjacent app does with tips: it repeats one feature from several angles across a week and triggers tips off what the user built, rather than sending a daily tip of the day. — [Sequenzy, Notion onboarding](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/brands/notion/sequences/onboarding); [Sequenzy blog](https://www.sequenzy.com/blog/successful-saas-email-marketing-examples)
- UNCONFIRMED ORIGIN: "63% of users say onboarding emails influence retention," "86% would stay loyal to a business that invests in onboarding content," and "apps with onboarding messages have 24% higher install-to-purchase conversion" circulate on vendor blogs without a traceable study. — [Userpilot, onboarding email best practices](https://userpilot.com/blog/onboarding-email-best-practices/); [Pushwoosh, increase app retention](https://www.pushwoosh.com/blog/increase-user-retention-rate/)

### Inferences
- Nothing found separates "tip" emails from "nudge" emails in retention effect. The captured consumer apps (Duolingo, Headspace, Calm) send far more nudges than tips, which suggests they have not found tip content to be the lever; where they teach, they teach one thing and make the button the thing itself.
- The PostHog result (fewer, more personal, single-ask emails outperform fuller ones) is the closest thing to an A/B result on length and is consistent with the reading-time data.

### Gaps
- Duolingo's and Headspace's published growth writing (Lenny's Newsletter, Growth.Design) concerns push notifications, streaks and in-app onboarding; no published Duolingo or Headspace data on email tips was found.
- RevenueCat's State of Subscription Apps and Amplitude's retention reports were not searched in depth; neither surfaced in searches for email-tip retention effects, and they report retention curves, not channel attribution.
- No controlled study of educational-email length vs return visits in a consumer app was found.

## Key Question 5: What do the best examples do with screenshots and GIFs?

### Takeaway
The captured consumer examples use one visual per email: Headspace an illustration or a single looping GIF that carries the whole message, Strava one hero image with the benefits beneath, Duolingo its character art. Product screenshots as such are rarer than illustration in the admired examples, and the one published test (PostHog) found illustration beat screenshots; where a screenshot is used the practitioner advice is to crop to the one region the email is about, serve it at 2x the display width, and keep the button directly under it.

### Cited Findings
- Headspace's education email is a single animated GIF (sun and moon) with a one-line message; the curator faults only the CTA being too low and low-contrast. — [Really Good Emails, Fit Headspace into any day](https://reallygoodemails.com/emails/fit-headspace-into-any-day-or-night/info)
- Headspace's welcome is "playful illustrations and clear CTAs." — [Really Good Emails, Welcome to Headspace](https://reallygoodemails.com/emails/welcome-to-headspace/info)
- Strava's welcome: a great image, then it "walks users through the benefits quickly." — [Really Good Emails, Welcome to Strava](https://reallygoodemails.com/emails/welcome-to-strava)
- PostHog replaced product screenshots with the team illustrator's art and the art version won. — [PostHog](https://posthog.com/blog/how-we-built-email-onboarding)
- Appcues: "Use screenshots, short videos, or step-by-step guides to visually demonstrate how users can benefit from different features." — [Appcues](https://www.appcues.com/blog/onboarding-users-with-email)
- NN/g: animation that demonstrates the product (the tear-away chinos) is the case where a GIF helped; decorative animation lowered value and trust. — [NN/g, Animated GIFs in email](https://nngroup.com/articles/gif-emails)
- Dell's XPS 12 GIF (laptop folding into tablet) is the standard case cited for a demonstrating GIF: +42% click rate, +103% conversion, +109% revenue. Reported by email vendors; Dell's original presentation not found online. UNCONFIRMED PRIMARY. — [Tuts+, animated GIFs in email](https://business.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-use-animated-gifs-to-boost-your-email-marketing-results--cms-27020)
- Image sizing: export at 2x the rendered width (1200px source for a 600px column; a 300px-wide phone crop from a 600px source); single column; 44px minimum button height. — [Bluleadz](https://www.bluleadz.com/blog/-how-to-retina-email-images); [Mailsoftly](https://mailsoftly.com/blog/email-design-best-practices/)
- Litmus's "Single CTA" layout classification shows up in 25% of Duolingo's captured designs. — [Sequenzy, Duolingo](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/brands/duolingo)
- Calm's critique and Headspace's critique both land on the same fault: the button too far down the email. — [Growth Snippets, Calm](https://growthsnippets.substack.com/p/every-email-calm-sent-me-after-i); [Really Good Emails, Headspace](https://reallygoodemails.com/emails/fit-headspace-into-any-day-or-night/info)

### Inferences
- For Lunch Sorted's library: one cropped screenshot per email of the single screen region the email is about (the Pack sheet, the kid's-pick grid, the shopping list), no full-phone frame unless the frame itself is the point, exported at twice its email width, with the button immediately beneath it so the F-pattern's top bar covers image and button together.
- A short GIF is justified only when it shows a gesture the parent would not guess (a swipe to trade a compartment, the kid's pick tap); a still is the default. Apple Mail plays GIFs; the Gmail app does too, but Outlook desktop shows the first frame, so the first frame must stand alone.
- A caption of one line under the image is consistent with NN/g's "headings and front-loading" guidance and with the captured examples, which caption with the benefit, not the feature name.

### Gaps
- No capture surfaced of Mealime, Instacart, HelloFresh, Cozi or Peloton education emails to check how they crop screenshots.
- No measured comparison of cropped-region screenshots vs full phone frames in email was found; the advice is practitioner consensus, not data.
- GIF autoplay support per client (Apple Mail, Gmail iOS, Outlook) was not re-verified from a 2025 to 2026 source in this pass.
