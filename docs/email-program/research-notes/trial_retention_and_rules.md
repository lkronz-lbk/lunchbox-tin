# Lifecycle email beyond onboarding: trial end, downgrade, win-back, dunning, digests, and the rules that bind them

Scope note for the writer: US market, 2023–2026. The reader runs a 21-day free trial of everything that drops to a free tier (nothing deleted, premium pieces pause), charges through Stripe on the web and the App Store on iPhone, sends through Resend, and uses no analytics SDKs. Where a source is a vendor blog or an aggregator rather than a primary document, that is said inline. Dates of retrieval: 2026-10-24.

## Key question 1: Trial-ending reminders — timing, count, content, and whether a reminder helps or hurts paid conversion

### Takeaway
The only controlled evidence found says a pre-trial-end reminder *raises* paid conversion, not lowers it (ShareFile A/B test: 62% vs 53% paid conversion; Blinkist: trial starts +23%, cancellations −4%), and the two billing platforms that matter here have fixed their own defaults at 7 days (Stripe's hosted email) and 3 days (Stripe's `trial_will_end` webhook). Captured real-world sequences cluster at 3 days / 2 days / 1 day ("tomorrow", "48 hours", "24 hours left"), with a single CTA and the price almost never shown in the reminder itself.

### Cited Findings

**Evidence that a reminder helps rather than hurts**
- ShareFile (2007, MarketingSherpa case study) A/B-tested a registration form with a pre-checked box offering "an automated email notification three days before the free trial expired, reminding users their account would convert to paid subscription and providing cancellation instructions." Registrations rose 16.4%, and "62% of trial users who registered with the reminder option converted to paid subscribers, compared to 53% without it." Six-week test, 90% confidence. — [MarketingSherpa](https://marketingsherpa.com/article/case-study/simple-email-cancellation-reminder-lifts)
- Blinkist: "When a mobile app called Blinkist started giving users the option to get notified before their trials ended, they saw a 23% increase in trials. … trial cancellations also decreased by 4%." (Indie Hackers post relaying Blinkist's own account; secondary source, the Blinkist original was not retrieved.) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tip-increase-trial-signups-by-notifying-users-when-their-trials-are-about-to-end-51d2e789bf)
- RevenueCat, State of Subscription Apps 2025 (75,000 apps, $10B+ tracked revenue): trial cancellations "spike toward trial end as users set reminders before charging"; 3-day and 7-day trials have the highest Day 0/Day 1 cancellations, 30-day trials spread cancellations across the period. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/)
- Baremetrics "pre-dunning" reminders (emails sent before a known card expiry/renewal; ~300,000 emails): 7 days before, 41.39% open / 10.74% click / 13.84% "recovery"; 30 days before, 47.41% open / 12.97% click / 14.37%. Published 2024-08-30, updated 2026-10-16. The closest large-sample data on *advance notice* emails; not a trial-specific study. — [Baremetrics](https://baremetrics.com/blog/improve-dunning-recovery-rate)

**Trial length and conversion context (RevenueCat 2025 unless noted)**
- Trials of 17–32 days have the highest median trial-to-paid conversion, 45.7% (the search summary of the same report gives 42.5% for 17–32 days vs 25.5% for trials under 4 days; the two figures come from different cuts of the report and the writer should quote 45.7% with "17–32 days, median" and treat the 42.5% as a second cut). — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/); second cut via [SaaStr summary](https://saastr.com/what-17000-subscription-apps-tell-us-about-free-trial-length-annual-plans-convert-86-better-with-30-day-trials-monthly-tops-out-at-two-weeks-and-ai-apps-hit-a-wall-at-16-days)
- Median hard-paywall app converts 10.7% of trials to paid by Day 35 versus 2.1% for freemium (relevant: Lunch Sorted is freemium after day 21). — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- Median trial-to-paid by category: Travel 48.7%, Media & Entertainment 43.8%, Health & Fitness 39.9%. 82% of trial starts happen the same day the app is installed. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/)
- Opt-in (no card) vs opt-out (card required) trials: opt-out trials convert at roughly 2.5–3× the rate of opt-in trials but attract 3–4× fewer sign-ups; "good" benchmarks around 25% opt-in and 60% opt-out. (Aggregator figures attributed loosely to ProfitWell; not a primary ProfitWell study.) — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/markdown/trial-conversion-rates-for-in-app-subscriptions.md)

**Platform defaults that set the de-facto timing**
- Stripe's hosted trial reminder: "turn on Send a reminder email 7 days before a free trial ends." If the trial is shorter than seven days the email goes out when the trial starts. When the trial ends without a payment method and the subscription is paused, the email's hosted page lets the customer add a card and activate. The hosted-page link expires "30 days since we sent the trial ending email." — [Stripe: Automate customer emails](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/revenue-recovery/customer-emails)
- Stripe's webhook for a self-sent reminder: `customer.subscription.trial_will_end` is "Sent according to the Trial ending events setting in the Dashboard, which defaults to 3 days before the trial ends. If the trial is shorter than the configured interval, it triggers immediately. … Use this event to send your own reminder email. Stripe-hosted reminder emails are sent 7 days before the trial ends." Stripe points to a "Manage compliance requirements for trials and promotions" page for legal obligations. — [Stripe: Configure trial offers](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/trials)
- California's Automatic Renewal Law (amended, effective 2025-07-01) requires a renewal notice only "for free or discounted trial periods lasting more than 31 days," sent at least 3 and no more than 21 days before the trial ends, stating renewal length, charges, frequency and how to cancel. A 21-day trial is below that threshold. California also now requires an annual reminder of any auto-renewal and 7–30 days' notice before a fee change. (Law-firm summaries of the statute; primary statute text not fetched.) — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-06-04-california-automatic-renewal-law-amendments-take-effect-on-july-1-2025); [California AG consumer alert](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-issues-consumer-alert-california%E2%80%99s-automatic-renewal-law)

**What captured trial-ending emails look like (brand, timing, content)**
- Headspace onboarding/trial flow (Reteno gallery capture; Headspace's standard trial is 7–14 days): +1h "Brighter days are here"; +1d "What matters to you?"; +4d "Start feeling happier today"; +6d "40% off"; +8d "One step at a time"; +9d "24 hours left". Pattern: a discount two to three days before the end, then a 24-hours-left urgency email. Price and lose-vs-keep content were not visible without login. — [Reteno gallery: Headspace](https://gallery.reteno.com/flows/emails-headspace)
- Headspace, captured on Really Good Emails (Jan 2023): subject "Your guide to a happier new year," preheader "30 days free ends soon," a 30-day-free offer with an expiry date (Jan 9, 2023). — [Really Good Emails](https://reallygoodemails.com/emails/your-guide-to-a-happier-new-year/text)
- Calm (Growth Snippets capture of a full year, 2020, after a non-trial sign-up): Day 0 "your discount is inside" (40% off); Day 1 "Following up on our offer"; Day 2 pain-point content; Day 4 seasonal content with "View Offer"; Day 10 feature + 40% CTA; then Day 65, 84, 93, 98, 135 content emails with "Upgrade and Unlock"/"Unlock All Sessions" CTAs. Dense for ten days, then one email every two to six weeks; discounts up front, features later. No trial-ending email in the capture. — [Growth Snippets](https://growthsnippets.substack.com/p/every-email-calm-sent-me-after-i)
- Duolingo (Sequenzy capture): onboarding Day 0 ×2, Day 1 "👀 Hmm. Interesting", Day 2 "💔 Don't go breaking my heart", Day 4 "Your English skills are getting moldy 🤢"; re-engagement Day 0 "🙏 Help me help you", Day 5 "Your last reminder from me" (i.e., two emails, then silence). 38% of sends on Tuesdays, ~9 PM UTC; median subject 23 characters; half use an emoji. No Super Duolingo trial-ending email in the capture. — [Sequenzy: Duolingo](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/brands/duolingo)
- Sequenzy's trial-ending collection (212 emails, 145 brands, mostly B2B SaaS) — representative subjects and timing: Novu "Your Pro trial ends in 2 days. Here is what you'll lose." (lists losses, one CTA); HelpDocs "Your trial ends tomorrow. What do you need?"; Yesware "…expires in 48 hours"; Nifty "ends in 3 days" then "ends tomorrow"; Sendbird "Your trial is ending, keep your free account!" (freemium framing: keep, not lose); Podpage "upgrade now and save $10". In every captured example the price is *not* shown in the reminder and there is one CTA. — [Sequenzy: trial-ending examples](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/trial-ending)
- Noom (14-day card-required trial): the BBB and class-action coverage note that "users who forget to cancel before the free trial ends will be charged without having received a separate reminder," and Noom's terms record that the trial "converts to paid without separate notice" — i.e., a well-known consumer app that chose *not* to send a reminder, and it became a $56M settlement and a BBB warning. — [Conduct Atlas (Noom ToS provision record)](https://conductatlas.com/platform/noom/noom-terms-of-service/free-trial-converts-to-paid-without-separate-notice/); [NC JOLT on the $56M settlement](https://journals.law.unc.edu/ncjolt/?p=8616)
- Vendor playbooks (not evidence, but the prevailing template): "7 days before, another a day before, with a text if the first email wasn't opened, and a final email when it ends" (Courier); "sequence typically starts 3–7 days before expiration and continues through the final day" (Sequenzy); "5–15% conversion on trial ending emails is typical" (Sequenzy, uncited). — [Courier recipe](https://www.courier.com/recipes/trial-ending-email-sequence); [Sequenzy templates](https://www.sequenzy.com/blog/trial-conversion-email-examples)

### Inferences
- For a 21-day opt-in trial that drops to a free tier, the data points toward two reminders at most (around day 14 or 18, and the day before), framed as "what pauses / what stays" rather than "you will be charged," because nothing will be charged. The Sendbird "keep your free account" framing is the freemium analogue of the card-required "you'll be charged" reminder.
- Showing the price inside the reminder is uncommon in the captures; the price lives on the landing page. For a dual-platform app this also sidesteps the iPhone/web price question (see Key question 6).
- The "reminder lowers conversion" worry has no published support that I could find; the two controlled or semi-controlled cases both went the other way, and RevenueCat's cancellation-spike data suggests users already set their own reminders, so the app's reminder mainly changes who gets the credit for the trust.

### Gaps
- No captured trial-ending email from Noom, Peloton, NYT, Spotify or Mealime was retrievable (Really Good Emails category pages returned "not found"; milled.com was not fetched). Noom is documented as sending none.
- No RevenueCat, Apple, Paddle/ProfitWell, Baremetrics, Chargebee or Recurly study isolates the effect of a trial-ending *email* on paid conversion; the Baremetrics pre-dunning numbers are the nearest large-sample proxy.
- Stripe's "Manage compliance requirements for trials and promotions" page was linked but not fetched; it may list jurisdictions where a reminder is mandatory.

## Key question 2: Trial-ended and downgrade emails for freemium apps (nothing deleted, features paused), and how many follow-ups before silence

### Takeaway
Freemium products that pause rather than delete describe the downgrade in help pages with the same three moves — "nothing is lost," "X becomes view-only," "upgrade to get it back" — and their captured re-engagement sequences stop after two to three emails (Duolingo: two emails in five days, the second titled "Your last reminder from me"). No vendor publishes an A/B result for the trial-ended email itself.

### Cited Findings
- Todoist, trial ends and downgrade: "your account will be downgraded to the Beginner plan and your access to certain features and usage limits will be reduced"; "your data will not be lost. However, if you have more projects than the free plan allows, Todoist will automatically make projects view-only according to the creation date … your 5 oldest projects will become view-only"; filter data "will be retained, however, you won't be able to view the filter content until you upgrade." — [Todoist Help: Start a Pro trial](https://www.todoist.com/help/account-and-billing/plans/start-a-todoist-pro-trial-LAVIT1Xm3); [Todoist Help: What happens when I downgrade](https://ai.todoist.com/help/articles/what-happens-when-i-downgrade-dOhlH9qQe)
- Notion, downgrade to Free: "All existing Custom Agents are switched off, but not deleted. You can view (but not edit)"; automations become "view-only"; nothing is deleted; a one-person workspace keeps unlimited blocks; "You'll receive confirmation of your downgrade, and see when your current plan ends." — [Notion Help: Plan downgrade](https://www.notion.com/help/plan-downgrade)
- Dropbox trial-ending emails ("Your free trial of analytics in Dropbox ends soon") drew forum complaints that they "create the impression that they will no longer be able to add new files and that upgrading is the only option" — a cautionary example of a downgrade email that over-states the loss. — [Dropbox Community](https://dropboxforum.com/discussions/101001018/i-received-an-email-that-my-trial-has-ended-but-i-have-a-paid-subscription/801749)
- Duolingo's re-engagement is two emails five days apart, the second "Your last reminder from me," then nothing. — [Sequenzy: Duolingo](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/brands/duolingo)
- Calm's post-sign-up sequence goes quiet after day 10 and resumes at one email every few weeks (days 65, 84, 93, 98, 135), content-led with an upgrade CTA. — [Growth Snippets](https://growthsnippets.substack.com/p/every-email-calm-sent-me-after-i)
- Stripe's own trial-ended behaviour for no-card trials: `trial_settings.end_behavior.missing_payment_method` is `create_invoice` (default; invoice fails, subscription goes `past_due`), `cancel`, or `pause` ("Pauses the subscription until you resume it"); `customer.subscription.paused` fires when a trial ends without a payment method and the behaviour is `pause`. — [Stripe: Configure trial offers](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/trials)
- RevenueCat: freemium apps convert a median 2.1% of trials by Day 35 (vs 10.7% hard paywall). — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- CAN-SPAM classes a message that "notifies the recipient about a change in the terms or features of a membership, subscription, account … or notifies the recipient of a change in their standing with respect to that ongoing commercial relationship" as transactional/relationship content — a plain "your trial ended, here is what paused" email fits; adding an upsell flips it to commercial if the subject line reads as a promotion or the transactional content is not at the top. — [FTC CAN-SPAM Compliance Guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)

### Inferences
- The help-page language from Todoist and Notion is the vocabulary parents will recognise: "kept," "view-only," "paused," "nothing deleted." A trial-ended email that lists what still works first and what paused second matches both the FTC placement test and the Dropbox lesson.
- The observed norm for follow-ups after a trial ends is short: two to three emails, then the account falls into the ordinary low-frequency cadence (Calm) or silence (Duolingo).

### Gaps
- No captured *email* (as opposed to help page) from Evernote, Notion, Dropbox, Todoist, Strava, Grammarly or Mealime announcing a trial's end was retrievable; wording above is from help centres and forum reports.
- No published count of "follow-ups before silence" from any benchmark report; the two-to-three figure is from captured sequences, not a study.

## Key question 3: Win-back and re-engagement cadence for consumer subscription apps — how many, over how long, discount or not, and the evidence

### Takeaway
The best-sourced figures say: stop after three emails (Klaviyo, 2017: revenue per recipient falls after the third), reactivation of lapsed subscribers is single-digit to low-teens by plan (RevenueCat 2025: monthly 13%+, weekly 9.4%, yearly 4.6%), and a 20% discount performed the same as 30% in RevenueCat's cited test — so discount size matters less than asking at all. Apple's own win-back offers surface automatically in-app and in the App Store for lapsed App Store subscribers, which does part of this work on iPhone without an email.

### Cited Findings
- Klaviyo win-back study (Q2 2017 data, published 2017-10-03): average open rate 32.09%; of the top 20 campaigns by revenue per recipient, "16 had three or fewer emails in the series"; "revenue per recipient begins dropping after three emails." Best subject lines: "It's been awhile" 35% open, "We miss you" 30.29%, "Discount included" 27.9%. (E-commerce, not apps; the only published multi-brand cadence study found.) — [Marketing Dive on Klaviyo](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/study-limit-win-back-email-campaigns-to-3-sends/506339/)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2025 reactivation rates for churned subscribers: monthly plans 13%+ across regions, weekly 9.37%, yearly 4.58%. "Nearly 30% of annual subscriptions are canceled in the first month." Year-1 retention: yearly 44.1%, monthly 17.0%, weekly 3.4%. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/)
- RevenueCat win-back guide (2024-06-27): "Reaching out too soon after a user has churned may be ineffective … waiting too long may result in the user losing interest"; push "can drive conversion up by more than 25%, however you shouldn't stop there"; a cited test "compared a 20% versus 30% discount and found both converted equally"; "Whenever we asked for help and offered a discount at the same time, we saw a higher engagement"; benchmark win-back 6% at month 2 and 8% at month 6 for a weak program vs 35% by month 6 for top performers; email should point to a web landing page with Stripe checkout. — [RevenueCat win-back guide](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/win-back-customers-how-to-guide.md)
- App Store win-back offers: eligibility is set by paid duration (1–24 months, or 3/4/5 years) and time since lapse (1–24 months); offers appear automatically on the Manage Subscriptions page (iOS 14.3+), as a sheet in the app (iOS 18+), on the product page and in editorial when promoted; "Apple does not directly message lapsed subscribers"; developers may share redemption URLs by email; types are pay-as-you-go, pay-up-front, free; limits 350 per subscription, 5 active per storefront. — [App Store Connect Help: Set up win-back offers](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/set-up-win-back-offers/); [Apple: Auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)
- Generic email win-back benchmarks (consultancy aggregate, 2025): "a well-run win-back email flow converts 2–5% of lapsed recipients; top-quartile programs hit 5–10%"; program-level reactivation across channels 12–20%, 20–35% for strong programs. (Not primary data.) — [EightX](https://eightx.co/blog/average-win-back-reactivation-rate-benchmarks)
- Omnisend lapsed-purchase automation averages (e-commerce): 33.0% open, 1.96% click-to-send, 0.52% conversion, ~$0.49 revenue per email. (Secondary citation.) — [Panoramata benchmark](https://www.panoramata.co/benchmark-marketing/winback-campaign)
- Vendor cadence advice: "3–5 emails over 1–2 weeks" (Substack playbook); "introduce incentives only after a value-led first touch" (Attribuly/Klaviyo guide); "start … slightly beyond the median time between orders, then add two more touches 10–15 days apart." — [Arijit Mondal](https://arijitmondal.substack.com/p/winback-email-sequence-7-high-converting); [Attribuly](https://attribuly.com/blogs/klaviyo-win-back-flow-best-practices/)
- Unsubscribe pressure to respect when planning a win-back: "Over half of U.S. consumers will unsubscribe if they receive four or more marketing messages from the same sender within a single month" (secondary citation of a consumer survey). — [EmailListValidation](https://emaillistvalidation.com/blog/unsubscribe-rate-by-send-frequency-how-often-is-too-often/)

### Inferences
- A three-email win-back — one value-led, one that asks why and offers something, one last word — over two to four weeks, then silence, is consistent with every source above and with the Duolingo/Calm captures.
- For a school-year product the natural win-back moments are calendar ones (late August, early January, after spring break) rather than N days after lapse; none of the sources address seasonal products, so this is inference.
- On iPhone, an App Store win-back offer does the discount without a steering question; on the web the same offer needs a Stripe coupon and the email can link to it directly (Key question 6).

### Gaps
- No Braze or Iterable 2023–2026 benchmark report with reactivation rates was retrievable; only vendor blog posts turned up.
- No published head-to-head of "with discount" vs "without discount" win-back for consumer apps; RevenueCat's 20% vs 30% test is the nearest.
- No source on when to stop emailing a lapsed free-tier user who never paid (as opposed to a lapsed subscriber).

## Key question 4: Payment-failed (dunning) emails — what Stripe sends and when, vendor cadence, and what recovers most

### Takeaway
Stripe will send one email per failed attempt (after each Smart Retry; default 8 tries in 2 weeks) with a hosted update-card link, plus optional renewal reminders and a one-month-ahead expiring-card email; Baremetrics' million-email dataset says the day-0 email does most of the work (13.25% recovery) and emails after day 7 recover a third as much, while Recurly recommends a 28-day window with about five touches spread across it. On iPhone, Apple runs its own retries and a 3/16/28-day grace period you configure.

### Cited Findings
**Stripe's built-in emails and retries**
- "Enable Send emails when card payments fail … Stripe automatically sends an email to your customer after each failed payment. The email lets your customer know that their recent subscription payment failed and gives them the opportunity to update their payment method so it can be retried successfully." Emails for failed payment, trial ending, renewal reminders and expiring cards "include a link to a page where the customer can update their payment method," destination either the Stripe Customer Portal or your own page; all use your branding settings. — [Stripe: Automate customer emails](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/revenue-recovery/customer-emails)
- Renewal reminders: "turn on Send emails about upcoming renewals," sent only for `charge_automatically` subscriptions, N days before as configured under "Upcoming renewal events"; the renewal date is shown in the customer's timezone. Expiring-card emails go "1 month before your customer's card on file expires." Email logs are kept 60 days and include "Ending subscription trials" and "Canceled subscriptions." — same page.
- Smart Retries: "The recommended default setting is 8 tries within 2 weeks," configurable windows 1 week to 2 months; hard declines (lost/stolen card, `authentication_required`, etc.) are not retried until a new card arrives; after the window the subscription can be set to cancel, mark unpaid, stay past-due, or pause; "After the final payment attempt, Stripe makes no further payment attempts." Custom schedules allow up to three retries. — [Stripe: Automate payment retries](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/revenue-recovery/smart-retries)

**Recovery data and vendor cadence**
- Baremetrics (1,000,000+ dunning emails; published 2024-08-30, updated 2026-10-16): open / click / recovery by days delinquent — day 0: 41.29% / 11.21% / 13.25%; day 3: 34.1% / 7.64% / 11.46%; day 7: 32.7% / 6.72% / 11.51%; day 15: 28.17% / 4.11% / 4.22%; day 20: 25.71% / 3.43% / 3.83%; day 30: 26.83% / 3.93% / 4.2%. Recommendation: "4–6 emails across 30 days minimum; first email on day of failure is most effective." Median recovery rate 47.6%, top performers 70–85%; "50% of customer churn" stems from declined cards (vendor's own claim). — [Baremetrics](https://baremetrics.com/blog/improve-dunning-recovery-rate)
- Recurly benchmarks: "Set to 28 days for monthly plans — this fits inside the 30-day billing cycle and gives Recurly's retry engine maximum time to recover"; up to 60 days for annual; a 28-day window "supports roughly 5 or more touch points"; "Spread emails across the window; don't stack them at the start"; "The default 10-day window leaves revenue on the table." — [Recurly docs: Renewal dunning benchmarks](https://docs.recurly.com/recurly-subscriptions/docs/navigate-scale-benchmarks-101-renewal-dunning)
- Aggregate claims circulating in 2025–2026 vendor content (unverified against primary data; use with care): "a single email recovers 12–18%, a 3-email sequence 20–25%, a 5-email sequence 28–35%." — [DEV Community post](https://dev.to/insightlab/the-dunning-email-sequence-that-recovers-30-of-failed-payments-2edh)

**Apple's side**
- Billing Grace Period: configurable 3, 16 or 28 days (weekly plans cap at 6); "lets subscribers whose auto-renewal failed due to a payment issue continue accessing your app's paid content for a period of time while Apple continues to attempt to collect payment"; your app must still read renewal status to detect the grace state. — [App Store Connect Help: Billing Grace Period](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/enable-billing-grace-period-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions)
- "Starting in iOS 16.4 and iPadOS 16.4, if a subscription doesn't successfully renew, a system-provided sheet appears in your app upon launch with a prompt that lets customers update the payment method for their Apple Account." — [Apple: Auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)

### Inferences
- With Stripe's per-failure email on and Smart Retries at the default, a parent already receives up to eight Stripe emails in two weeks; a founder-written dunning email should replace, not add to, those (turn Stripe's off and send your own off `invoice.payment_failed`, or keep Stripe's and send nothing). Baremetrics' curve says the first two matter most.
- Apple handles the iPhone case end to end (retries, grace period, the in-app sheet); an email from the developer there is optional and should only say "update your card in Settings," never link to web checkout for the same subscription.

### Gaps
- Stripe does not publish how many failed-payment emails it sends per invoice beyond "after each failed payment," nor the exact copy; the email template was not retrievable.
- Chargebee's dunning cadence documentation was not fetched.
- Apple's "Reducing involuntary subscriber churn" page returned no body text; the 60-day billing retry period and whether Apple emails the customer during it could not be confirmed from a primary page.

## Key question 5: Weekly nudge or digest emails in meal-planning and family apps — who sends one, when, what is in it, and the evidence on frequency

### Takeaway
Among the named apps, Cozi (weekly agenda, Sundays, opt-in per family member) and eMeals (weekly menu by email on a day the user picks; new plans publish Wednesday mornings) are the documented senders; a 26K-subscriber meal-plan newsletter sends Fridays with a five-day plan, list and recipes. MailerLite's 2025 data over 1.4M campaigns shows weekly sends carry a lower unsubscribe rate (0.38%) than monthly (0.54%) or irregular (0.87%) ones.

### Cited Findings
- Cozi: weekly agenda emails "arrive once a week on Sundays," enabled per family member under Settings → "Daily and weekly agenda emails" (iOS) or "Email notifications" (desktop); "Add any changes you want by Saturday night and they will show up in the weekly email." Meals added to the calendar appear in the agenda emails. Cozi's blog variously describes the weekly email as "early Sunday morning" and "every Sunday night." — [Cozi: Be prepared with agenda emails](https://www.cozi.com/blog/be-prepared-with-agenda-emails/); [Cozi: weekly plan](https://www.cozi.com/blog/wish-you-had-instant-way-tell-everyone-familys-weekly-plan/); [Cozi Meal Planner](https://www.cozi.com/blog/cozi-meal-planner/)
- eMeals: "You can have the primary meal plan you subscribe to emailed directly to you each week. eMeals publishes new plans on Wednesday mornings into the app, but you can opt into having the email sent to you on any day you wish." ### Inferences
- The family-app convention is a Sunday weekly digest the parent opts into per person, built from what is already in the app (Cozi) — a "your week's lunches, your list" email on Sunday afternoon follows that pattern. eMeals' "pick your day" setting is the other documented model.
- A weekly digest is a "subscribed message" in Gmail's terms (Key question 6), so it needs one-click unsubscribe headers even though Lunch Sorted is far below the bulk threshold; Resend's transactional API does not add them for you.
- No source measures churn effects of a weekly digest in a subscription app specifically; the MailerLite figures are for marketing newsletters.

### Gaps
- Mealime, Plan to Eat, Paprika and Samsung Food: no documentation of a weekly email, its day or contents was found (Paprika has no server-side email; Samsung Food+ advertises AI weekly plans in-app). Treat as "not documented," not "does not send."
- No study links weekly digest sends to subscription churn or retention in consumer apps.

## Key question 6: The rules — Apple steering and email after the 2025 US rulings, Apple's own notices, Stripe's emails, CAN-SPAM, Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender rules, and Resend's support

### Takeaway
As of 2026-10-24 Apple's live guideline says developers "can send communications outside of the app to their user base about purchasing methods other than in-app purchase," and on the US storefront the in-app anti-steering ban "does not apply" — but the contempt ruling that forced this is now before the Supreme Court (cert granted 2026-06-30), so the US carve-out could narrow. Apple itself documents no pre-trial-end email for third-party app trials (only for price rises), so do not rely on one. Gmail/Yahoo's one-click-unsubscribe and 0.3% spam rules formally bind only senders near 5,000 messages a day, but authentication and the spam ceiling apply to everyone, and Resend adds the headers only on Broadcasts, not on API sends.

### Cited Findings

**Apple — what the guideline says now**
- 3.1.3 (live text, retrieved 2026-10-24): "Apps in this section cannot, within the app, encourage users to use a purchasing method other than in-app purchase, except for apps on the United States storefront and as set forth in 3.1.1(a) and 3.1.3(a). Developers can send communications outside of the app to their user base about purchasing methods other than in-app purchase." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.1(a): entitlements "are not required for developers to include buttons, external links, or other calls to action in their United States storefront apps"; "In all other storefronts, except for the United States storefront, where this prohibition does not apply, apps and their metadata may not include buttons, external links, or other calls to action that direct customers to purchasing mechanisms other than in-app purchase." — same page.
- 3.1.1 on free trials for *non-subscription* apps: "Prior to the start of the trial, your app must clearly identify its duration, the content or services that will no longer be accessible when the trial ends, and any downstream charges." (Not directly applicable to an auto-renewable subscription trial, but the only Apple text on disclosing what ends.) — same page.
- The May 2025 change: posted 2025-05-02, the changes were "The prohibition on encouraging users to use a purchasing method other than in-app purchase does not apply on the United States storefront" and "The External Link Account entitlement is not required for apps on the United States storefront to include buttons, external links, or other calls to action." — [512 Pixels, 2025-05-02](https://512pixels.net/2025/05/apple-updates-app-review-guidelines/)
- The earlier 3.1.3 sentence forbidding use of "information obtained within the app to target individual users outside the app to use purchasing methods other than in-app purchase (such as sending an individual user an email about other purchasing methods)" was removed in that update (law-firm summary). — [Shibolet](https://www.shibolet.com/en/apple-guidelines-update-redirecting-users-to-external-payment-mechanism/)

**Apple — the litigation timeline and what is uncertain**
- 2025-04-30: Judge Gonzalez Rogers found Apple in willful violation of the 2021 injunction (27% commission on external purchases, restrictions on how developers could communicate alternatives). — [Frankfurt Kurnit](https://fkks.com/news/court-finds-apple-violated-order-resulting-in-key-changes-for-ios-external-purchase-methods)
- 2025-12-11: Ninth Circuit unanimously affirmed the civil contempt finding, upheld the bar on Apple limiting "the design or placement of steering links," but held that "although Apple should be allowed to charge some fees on linked transactions, those should be limited and tied to the costs of any services Apple provides," and remanded on the commission. Rehearing en banc denied 2026-03-30. — [Cravath](https://www.cravath.com/news-insights/epic-games-ninth-circuit-win-affirming-civil-contempt-finding.html); [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/06/30/high-court-grants-cert-in-apples-challenge-to-ninth-circuit-contempt-ruling-in-app-store-dispute/)
- 2026-06-30: the Supreme Court granted certiorari on (1) whether contempt can rest on an order's "spirit" rather than "a clear and unambiguous violation of the order's text," and (2) whether the Ninth Circuit disregarded *Trump v. CASA* limits on relief. No stay of the injunction or of the guideline carve-out was reported in the sources fetched; argument and decision dates not yet set. — [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/06/30/high-court-grants-cert-in-apples-challenge-to-ninth-circuit-contempt-ruling-in-app-store-dispute/); [MacRumors, 2026-09-14](https://macrumors.com/2026/09/14/apple-supreme-court-contempt-ruling)
- What this means for email, stated plainly: the sentence permitting out-of-app communications about other purchase methods is global and pre-dates 2025 in substance; the *in-app* US carve-out and the removal of the "target individual users by email" clause are the 2025 changes, and those are the parts a Supreme Court reversal could unwind. An email that names the web price and links to Stripe checkout is permitted today under 3.1.3; whether it remains so after the Court rules is the open question.

**Apple — does Apple itself email before a trial ends?**
- Apple's subscriptions page documents automatic customer messaging only for price increases: "When you increase the price of an auto-renewable subscription, Apple automatically provides push notifications, email, and in-app messaging." It says nothing about free-trial or introductory-offer expiry. — [Apple: Auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)
- The App Store Connect help page on introductory offers lists trial lengths (3 days; 1 or 2 weeks; 1, 2, 3 or 6 months; 1 year) and contains no notification promise. — [App Store Connect Help: Introductory offers](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/set-up-introductory-offers-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions)
- Apple's developer forum thread asking exactly this (2018) has no Apple answer, only conflicting developer assumptions. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 95882](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/95882)
- Press reports of Apple emailing and pushing "Expiring Soon" reminders concern Apple's *own* services (Apple Music 2015, Apple TV+ free year 2020), not third-party apps. — [MacTrast 2015](https://www.mactrast.com/2015/09/apple-notifies-apple-music-users-about-renewals-as-free-trial-nears-end/amp/); [MacTrast 2020](https://www.mactrast.com/2020/01/apple-reminding-customers-to-redeem-their-free-year-of-apple-tv/)
- Apple does automatically surface App Store win-back offers to lapsed subscribers in-app and on the Manage Subscriptions page, but "Apple does not directly message lapsed subscribers." — [App Store Connect Help: win-back offers](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/set-up-win-back-offers/)

**Stripe — built-in emails (summary; detail in Key question 4)**
- Trial reminder fixed at 7 days before; renewal reminders at a configurable lead; failed-payment email after each failed attempt; expiring-card email one month ahead; all with a hosted update-payment link (Customer Portal or your URL) and your branding; a "Manage subscription" link can be added to emails; logs include "Ending subscription trials" and "Canceled subscriptions." — [Stripe: Automate customer emails](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/revenue-recovery/customer-emails)
- `customer.subscription.trial_will_end` defaults to 3 days before; Stripe explicitly offers it so you can "send your own reminder email." — [Stripe: Configure trial offers](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/trials)

**CAN-SPAM — transactional vs commercial**
- Transactional/relationship content: content that "facilitates, completes, or confirms a commercial transaction that the recipient already has agreed to"; "notifies the recipient about a change in the terms or features of a membership, subscription, account, loan or other ongoing commercial relationship"; "notifies the recipient of a change in their standing with respect to that ongoing commercial relationship." — [FTC CAN-SPAM Compliance Guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)
- Mixed messages: "If a recipient reasonably interpreting the subject line would likely conclude that the message contains an advertisement or promotion … or if the message's transactional or relationship content does not appear mainly at the beginning of the message, the primary purpose of the message is commercial." — same.
- Commercial messages must: not use false headers or deceptive subjects, identify the message as an ad, give a valid physical postal address, offer a clear opt-out that works for at least 30 days, honour opt-outs within 10 business days, and the sender is liable for vendors acting on its behalf. Penalty up to $53,088 per email. — same.
- Application (inference from the text): a trial-ending email whose subject is "Your free three weeks end Friday — here is what pauses" is a change-in-standing notice; "Last chance: 30% off before your trial ends" is commercial and needs the address, the opt-out and the ad identification. A weekly digest the parent opted into is "subscribed" marketing for Gmail purposes and commercial for CAN-SPAM unless it only reports the parent's own plan.
- Related: the FTC's "click-to-cancel" (negative option) rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit on 2025-07-08, days before its 2025-07-14 effective date, on procedural grounds; state auto-renewal laws (California's above) still apply. — [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/07/ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule-vacated-ahead-of-planned-july-14-effective-date)

**Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules**
- All senders to Gmail (from 2024-02-01): SPF or DKIM; valid PTR; TLS; RFC 5322 formatting; keep spam rate "below 0.3%" in Postmaster Tools. — [Google: Email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)
- Bulk senders: anyone who "sends close to 5,000 messages or more to personal Gmail accounts within a 24-hour period," counted across the same primary domain; "Senders who meet the above criteria at least once are permanently considered bulk senders." Bulk senders need SPF *and* DKIM, a DMARC record (p=none suffices), From-domain alignment, and one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058 `List-Unsubscribe` + `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) honoured within two days — applying to "marketing and promotional messages"; "Transactional messages like password resets are excluded." Spam-rate bands: below 0.1% compliant; 0.1–0.29% hurts delivery; 0.3%+ ineligible for mitigation. Timeline: Feb 2024 start, April 2024 error codes, June 2024 mitigation cut-off, and "November 2025: Gmail is ramping up its enforcement on non-compliant traffic." Non-compliant mail gets "Temporary or Permanent Failure codes, or spam foldering." — [Google: Sender guidelines FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414)
- Yahoo (from February 2024): "Implement both SPF & DKIM"; "Publish a valid DMARC policy with at least p=none"; "Implement a functioning list-unsubscribe header, which supports one-click unsubscribe" and "Honor unsubscribes within 2 days"; "Keep your spam rate below 0.3%"; valid forward and reverse DNS. Yahoo's page states no volume threshold. — [Yahoo Sender Hub](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/)

**Resend**
- Free plan: 100 emails a day, 3,000 a month, 3 domains, 1,000 contacts, 30-day data retention. Pro: $20/mo for 50,000 emails ($35 for 100,000), no daily limit, overage $0.90 per 1,000. Marketing (Broadcasts) plans are priced by contacts ($40/mo for 5,000) and "not limited by the number of emails sent." Dedicated IPs $30/mo on Scale for senders above 3,000 a day. — [Resend pricing](https://resend.com/pricing); [Resend sending limits](https://resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/resend-sending-limits)
- One-click unsubscribe on API (transactional) sends: you pass `headers: { 'List-Unsubscribe': '<https://…/unsubscribe>', 'List-Unsubscribe-Post': 'List-Unsubscribe=One-Click' }` yourself; Resend does not host the endpoint — yours must answer POST with 200/202 and a blank page, serve a GET form, and stop mail "within 48 hours of this request." "Resend doesn't manage contact lists for transactional emails"; Broadcasts "add the List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers for you" and manage topics, segments and preferences. — [Resend: Add unsubscribe to transactional emails](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/add-unsubscribe-to-transactional-emails); [Resend: Unsubscribe page](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/settings/unsubscribe-page)
- Project memory (not a web source): the Resend free plan's 100-a-day cap binds before any sign-in rate limit, and the daily cron for trial emails already runs on it — a weekly digest to every household would need to fit under the same 100 or move to Pro.

### Inferences
- Today an iPhone-origin household may lawfully receive an email that says "the yearly plan is $X on the web" with a Stripe link; the safer long-run framing, given the pending Supreme Court case, is to keep price and checkout on the web page the email links to, and to keep the email itself a notice (what pauses, what stays, one link). That also keeps it transactional under CAN-SPAM.
- Because the trial is 21 days and no card is taken, neither California's ARL trial-notice rule nor Stripe's 7-day hosted reminder applies; the reminder is a product choice, not a compliance one, which is why the ShareFile/Blinkist evidence (conversion goes up) is the operative argument.
- Lunch Sorted will not be a Gmail "bulk sender" at current volume, but the all-sender rules (SPF/DKIM, 0.3% spam) already apply, DMARC p=none costs nothing, and adding RFC 8058 headers to the digest and win-back sends now avoids becoming permanently classified as bulk without the headers later. Trial-ending and dunning notices can stay header-free as transactional.
