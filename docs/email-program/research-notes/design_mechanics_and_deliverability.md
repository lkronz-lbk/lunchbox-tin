# Design mechanics and deliverability for a phone-first lifecycle email program (Resend, US parents on iPhone)

Research date: 2026-10-24. Every figure carries the year its source gives. "Folklore" marks a claim that circulates widely but that I could not trace to a primary, dated source. Secondary sources (vendor blogs, help centers) are labelled as such.

Who reads the mail (frames everything below):

- Litmus Email Client Market Share, September 2026, "calculated from over 1.5 billion opens": Apple 76.56%, Gmail 16.10%, Outlook 3.48%, Yahoo Mail 2.35%, Google Android 0.94%. Apple is one bucket because "Starting with Apple iOS 15 (iPhone), iPad OS 15, and Mac OS Monterey, opens from users opted into Apple's Mail Privacy Protection do not pass along specifics of the email client version and platform." The same page: "Studies show that Apple's MPP now impact roughly 55-60% of all email opens." — [Litmus](https://www.litmus.com/email-client-market-share)
- Design implication for this program: Apple Mail (iOS) and the Gmail iOS app are the two renderers that matter; Outlook for Windows is a rounding error for US parents on iPhones, so Outlook-specific hacks (VML buttons, MSO conditionals, GIF first-frame planning) can be reduced to "degrades gracefully," not "pixel-perfect."

## 1. Layout: width, columns, padding, type sizes, button height; is "hero screenshot + headline + one button" supported by data?

### Takeaway
Current guidance converges on a single column, 600px (or narrower) container, body text at 16px or larger, headings 22px+, line height around 1.5, and tap targets of at least 44px; WCAG 2.2 formalizes 24px (AA) / 44px (AAA) targets and 80-character lines. No primary study of "hero screenshot + headline + one button" as a layout exists; the closest evidence is indirect (CTA buttons rated most effective by marketers; NN/g finding that decorative animation lowers trust).

### Cited Findings
- Mailchimp's Email Design Reference (undated, long-standing): fixed-width mobile option 320px, responsive breakpoint 480px; minimum body font 16px, noting "Apple recommending 17-22px, Google recommending 18-22px"; tap targets "at least 46px squared" (splitting Apple's 44px and Google's 48px); design mantra "one eyeball, one thumb, and arm's-length." — [Mailchimp Email Design Reference](https://templates.mailchimp.com/design/mobile-friendliness/)
- 600px as the de facto container width, single column, breakpoint at 480px; body minimum 14–16px; headlines 22px+; buttons at least 44px tall, 200–300px wide, full-width on mobile; line height 1.4–1.6x. (Secondary: vendor roundups, 2025.) — [MessageFlow 2025](https://messageflow.com/?p=20423); [Zoho ZeptoMail](https://www.zoho.com/zeptomail/articles/email-template-size.html); [Hostinger](https://www.hostinger.com/tutorials/?p=144844)
- WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), Level AA: "The size of the target for pointer inputs is at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels," with exceptions for spacing, equivalent controls, inline links in text, user-agent controls, and essential layouts. The stricter SC 2.5.5 Target Size (Enhanced), Level AAA, is 44 by 44 CSS pixels. — [W3C Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html); [W3C Understanding 2.5.5](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html)
- WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.8 Visual Presentation (AAA) for blocks of text: "Width is no more than 80 characters or glyphs (40 if CJK)"; "Text is not justified"; "Line spacing (leading) is at least space-and-a-half within paragraphs, and paragraph spacing is at least 1.5 times larger than the line spacing"; text resizable to 200% without horizontal scrolling. — [W3C Understanding 1.4.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/visual-presentation.html)
- Litmus survey, January 2025 (reported by eMarketer): 35% of marketers worldwide name interactive buttons/CTAs the most effective interactive element; 23% report email CTRs above 5%. — [eMarketer citing Litmus](https://www.emarketer.com/content/ctas-lead-way-email-engagement-tactics)
- Gmail clips HTML over 102 KB with "[Message Clipped] View entire message"; the measure is raw HTML size (markup, inline CSS, URLs, tracking code), not images; a clipped email hides anything below the cut, including footer unsubscribe links. (Vendor help centers; the 102 KB figure is consistent across them.) — [ActiveCampaign help](https://help.activecampaign.com/hc/en-us/articles/115001060524); [Zoho Campaigns](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/campaigns-3-0/deliverability-guide/best-practices/articles/v3-how-to-prevent-gmail-from-clipping-your-emails)
- Nielsen Norman Group GIF study: 14 marketing emails shown animated to 55 respondents and static (single frame) to 66; respondents "chose more negative words and fewer positive ones for the animated versions" (p < 0.005) and rated static versions slightly more trustworthy. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gif-emails/)

### Inferences
- For a 390px-wide iPhone viewport, a 600px fluid table that collapses to 100% width with 20–24px side padding gives a text measure of roughly 40–55 characters at 16–17px, which sits comfortably under WCAG's 80-character ceiling; a fixed 600px layout without a mobile query would be scaled down by Apple Mail and make 16px text render smaller than 16px, so the mobile query is not optional.
- Match the app's own rules: the product already enforces 44px controls and AAA contrast; the email should use the same 44px (ideally 48px) button height, 16–17px body, 22–28px headline, and full-width buttons under 480px. That meets WCAG 2.5.5 (AAA), not just 2.5.8.
- "Hero screenshot + headline + one button" is a reasonable default for a lifecycle email because it keeps the HTML far under Gmail's 102 KB clip, puts one tappable target above the fold on a phone, and is what Apple Mail's and Gmail's preview panes show first, but this is design reasoning, not measured data (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No Litmus, Email on Acid, Campaign Monitor or Really Good Emails study was found that measures a single-image/single-CTA layout against alternatives. The widely repeated "a single CTA increased clicks 371% and sales 1617%" line (usually credited to WordStream/Campaign Monitor, c. 2016) is folklore: one unverifiable case, never a controlled study; do not cite it.
- The "responsive emails see 15% higher click-to-open rates" line attributed to Litmus's State of Email appeared only second-hand (Hostinger); I could not locate the Litmus original and its year.
- Really Good Emails' own design notes were not reached in this session.

## 2. Screenshots in email: cropping, phone frame, pixel width and retina, file size, GIF support, alt text, image blocking; any data on screenshots/GIFs lifting clicks

### Takeaway
Serve images at 2x the displayed CSS width with explicit width/height attributes, keep each under roughly 200 KB, and treat the screenshot as decoration that must have a text equivalent: Outlook desktop and AOL still block images by default, Gmail proxies them, and 51% of emails in the 2025 EMC corpus lacked alt text. Animated GIF plays in Apple Mail and Gmail; only classic Outlook for Windows shows the first frame. Evidence that GIFs raise clicks is vendor case-study folklore; the one controlled study (NN/g) found animation lowered trust.

### Cited Findings
- Retina guidance: provide a 2x asset (for a 600px slot, a 1200px file) and display at half its pixel dimensions; keep hero images under ~200 KB and inline images under ~80 KB. (Secondary, 2024–25.) — [MergeImages](https://mergeimages.net/blog/image-optimization-email-marketing); [TinyCommand docs](https://tinycommand.com/docs/emails/blocks/image)
- Image blocking (Litmus guide, published 2017, last updated August 2021): blocks images by default: Outlook 2007–2019, Outlook (Mac), Office 365 desktop (Windows and Mac), AOL webmail; shows by default: Gmail, Yahoo Mail, Outlook.com, Office 365 webmail, Apple Mail, Windows 10 Mail, and every tested mobile client (Gmail, AOL, Yahoo, iOS Mail, Samsung Mail, Outlook). Clients that show images "use image proxy servers to host the images... to check for viruses and malware." Nearly all clients render plain alt text when images are blocked; styled alt text fails in Outlook 2007–2019. — [Litmus image blocking guide](https://www.litmus.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-email-image-blocking); [Litmus styled alt text](https://www.litmus.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-styled-alt-text-in-email/)
- Gmail has displayed images by default through its own proxy (googleusercontent.com) since late 2013; images fail to load if the origin blocks the proxy's user agent or enforces strict referrer rules. (Secondary.) — [Latenode community](https://community.latenode.com/t/gmail-not-displaying-images-in-html-email-while-other-clients-work-fine/31251)
- Animated GIF support (Can I Email; page states "last tested" February 6, 2020 but carries entries dated to 2024): full support in Apple Mail (macOS 13+, iOS 13+), Gmail on all platforms (since February 2020), Yahoo, AOL, Samsung Email, Outlook iOS/Android; Outlook.com / new Outlook since January 2024; Outlook for Mac since 16.80; Outlook for Windows 2007–2019 shows only the first frame; Outlook 365 for Windows plays the animation three times then shows a play button. — [Can I Email: GIF](https://www.caniemail.com/features/image-gif/)
- Dark-mode image guidance (Litmus, February 27, 2025): use transparent PNGs with a translucent outline or glow for logos, swap images with `.dark-img`/`.light-img` classes under `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)`, add `[data-ogsc]` prefixes for Outlook apps, prefer midtone colors, and "Use APNGs instead of GIFs for better transparency handling." — [Litmus dark mode guide](https://www.litmus.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-dark-mode-for-email-marketers)
- Email Markup Consortium 2025 report: images missing alternate text in 51.42% of 443,585 emails. — [EMC 2025](https://emailmarkup.org/en/reports/accessibility/2025/)
- GIF click-lift claims: Dell XPS 12 campaign "42% increase in click rate, 103% boost in conversion rate" (2012, vendor case study, repeated endlessly); "up to 42%", "up to 22%", Experian "72% of clients see a lift" — all vendor-reported, none controlled. Folklore. — [FulcrumTech](https://fulcrumtech.net/blog/how-to-use-animated-gifs-in-email-marketing/); [Blings](https://www.blings.io/blog/technical/do-gifs-improve-email-click-through-rate/)
- The controlled counter-evidence: NN/g's A/B of animated vs. static frames found more negative word choice for animated versions (p < 0.005) and slightly lower trust. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gif-emails/)

### Inferences
- The 750x1624 library is already 2x for a 375px-wide displayed image; a 1624px-tall screenshot shown at 375x812 CSS px would occupy two phone screens and push the headline and button below the fold. Crop to the region the email is about (a compartment row, the kid's-pick card, the shopping list) and show it at 280–320px wide so the headline and button are visible without scrolling. No source measured "phone frame vs. no frame"; the frame adds width and bytes and shows nothing the parent does not already know (they are holding the phone), so the reasoning favors a bare, rounded-corner crop.
- Because MPP pre-fetches images and Gmail proxies them, the image request tells you nothing reliable about the person; treat images as pure presentation and never as a measurement channel.
- A short APNG or a two-frame GIF of a swap or a pick could be useful for the "what the kid does" email, but given NN/g and the Outlook first-frame rule, make the first frame complete and informative and keep the loop short; the composed store creatives (headline baked into the image) should be avoided because text in images disappears when images are blocked, cannot be resized, is not read by screen readers, and inverts unpredictably in Gmail's dark mode.

### Gaps
- No study found that measures app screenshots (as opposed to GIFs or hero photos) in onboarding emails.
- Can I Email's GIF page shows a 2020 "last tested" stamp while listing 2024 Outlook changes; the exact test dates per client were not retrieved.
- Apple Mail's handling of very large images (downscaling, memory limits) was not researched.

## 3. Dark mode: how each client treats it; the meta tags; keeping logos and screenshots right; 2024–2026 state of support

### Takeaway
Apple Mail honors `prefers-color-scheme` and leaves the design alone unless you opt in with the color-scheme meta tags; the Gmail iOS app ignores the media query and fully inverts, Gmail Android partially inverts, Gmail web does nothing; Outlook apps partially invert with `[data-ogsc]` hooks. Design for both outcomes: declare `color-scheme: light dark`, give every text its own color (never rely on default black), and use transparent PNG logos with an outline.

### Cited Findings
- Litmus per-client table (February 27, 2025): Apple Mail iOS/macOS "No change" (pure white #ffffff backgrounds inverted if the meta tags are present); Gmail iOS "Full invert, no @media support" (blend-mode workaround); Gmail Android "Partial invert, no @media support"; Gmail web "No change"; Outlook iOS and Android "Partial invert, no @media support, [data-ogsc] partial support"; Outlook Windows "Full invert"; Outlook macOS "Partial invert, partial @media support"; Outlook.com "Partial invert." Required head code: `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">`, `<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light dark">`, and `:root { color-scheme: light dark; }`, then `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) { ... !important }`. — [Litmus dark mode guide](https://www.litmus.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-dark-mode-for-email-marketers)
- Can I Email `@media (prefers-color-scheme)` (last tested March 8, 2023; 41.86% overall support): supported in Apple Mail (macOS, iOS 12.2+), Samsung Email Android 6.0+, Thunderbird, ProtonMail, Orange, SFR; "partially supported" rows for every Gmail and Outlook platform; not supported in Yahoo and AOL (the query is "transformed into @media ( _filtered_a )"), Fastmail (`@media none`), HEY (`@media (false)`). — [Can I Email: prefers-color-scheme](https://www.caniemail.com/features/css-at-media-prefers-color-scheme/)
- Conflict: Litmus (2025) says Gmail has "no @media support" for dark mode, while Can I Email's 2023 page lists Gmail as "partial"; secondary write-ups in 2024–25 side with Litmus: Gmail "doesn't support the color-scheme CSS property, the color-scheme meta tag, or the light-dark() function" and applies its own transformation, with Android doing a partial inversion. — [dev.to (pkrupar)](https://dev.to/pkrupar/dark-mode-in-html-email-everything-you-need-to-know-3k29); [Courier blog](https://www.courier.com/blog/dark-mode-email-design)
- Apple Mail on iOS supports `@media (prefers-color-scheme)` from iOS 13 and the `color-scheme` CSS property from iOS 16.1. (Secondary.) — [Courier blog](https://www.courier.com/blog/dark-mode-email-design)
- Dark-mode usage: Litmus reports "an average of 35% used Dark Mode in 2022." The "over 80% use dark mode on at least one device" figure that appears in vendor posts is unattributed. Folklore. — [Litmus dark mode guide](https://www.litmus.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-dark-mode-for-email-marketers)
- Image handling in dark mode: transparent PNG with translucent outline/glow; swap images via classes under the media query; midtones; APNG over GIF for transparency. — [Litmus dark mode guide](https://www.litmus.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-dark-mode-for-email-marketers)

### Inferences
- For an Apple-majority audience, the meta tags plus a hand-designed dark palette (the app already has one in its `:root` tokens) will be honored by roughly three-quarters of opens; the Gmail iOS app will invert whatever you send, so the dark palette must also survive inversion: avoid pure white text on colored buttons that invert to pale-on-pale, and check the inverted rendering of brand colors against 7:1.
- Screenshots: a light-mode screenshot on a dark Gmail iOS background looks like a bright card; that is acceptable. A dark-mode screenshot inverted by Gmail becomes a washed-out light image; so pick the light screenshot as the default and swap in the dark one only under the media query (Apple Mail), never as the baseline.
- The logo should be a transparent PNG with a 1px outline in a midtone, or a wordmark set in live HTML text with the brand font falling back cleanly (see 4), so inversion cannot break it.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 Can I Email retest of Gmail dark mode was found; the Litmus February 2025 table is the newest client-by-client source located.
- Whether Gmail iOS still fully inverts in its 2026 builds was not confirmed by a primary test; verify with a Litmus/Email on Acid preview before launch.

## 4. Web fonts: who loads Google Fonts; fallback stacks; reasons not to load them

### Takeaway
Apple Mail (iOS and macOS) and Samsung Email load `@font-face`/`@import` fonts; Gmail, Yahoo and AOL never do; Outlook for Windows reads the declaration, ignores the remote file and, if the custom font leads the stack, falls to Times New Roman. For this program, roughly three-quarters of opens could show Familjen Grotesk and Karla, but loading them from fonts.googleapis.com sends every open's IP address to Google, which contradicts a "nothing leaves the phone, no analytics SDKs" promise; either self-host the woff2 on lunchsorted.app or ship system fonts.

### Cited Findings
- Can I Email `@font-face` (last updated December 19, 2023; 24.39% support): partial support in Apple Mail macOS and iOS; supported in Outlook.com, ProtonMail, Fastmail, HEY, GMX/WEB.DE/Mail.ru; partial in Samsung Email Android 6.0 (except Microsoft accounts); Outlook for Windows 2003–2019 "the declaration is supported but distant fonts are ignored"; not supported in Gmail (web, iOS, Android, mobile webmail), Yahoo, AOL, Thunderbird. Advice for Outlook: "use mso-generic-font-family and mso-font-alt to control the fallback." — [Can I Email: @font-face](https://www.caniemail.com/features/css-at-font-face/)
- "If you include a custom font at the top of your font stack, Outlook will ignore all of your fallback fonts and instead display Times New Roman." Outlook 365 for Mac and the Outlook iPhone app do render Google Fonts; Gmail and Yahoo mobile apps do not. (Secondary.) — [Courier: fonts in email](https://www.courier.com/blog/fonts-in-email-what-works-what-breaks-and-how-to-fix-it); [Dialog Insight](https://support.dialoginsight.com/en/support/solutions/articles/1000293904-google-font-support-in-emails)
- Privacy: LG München I, 20 January 2022 (3 O 17493/20) awarded EUR 100 because loading Google Fonts from Google's servers transmitted the visitor's dynamic IP address to Google without consent; the court reasoned that a dynamic IP is personal data (CJEU Breyer) and that self-hosting is a cost-free, privacy-neutral alternative. — [activeMind.legal](https://www.activemind.legal/guides/ruling-google-fonts/); [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2022/01/german-court-rules-websites-embedding.html)

### Inferences
- A `<link>` or `@import` to fonts.googleapis.com in an email is functionally an open beacon to a third party: Apple Mail (via MPP's proxy, so Apple hides the IP) and Samsung fetch it on open; Gmail strips it. Self-hosting the three woff2 files on lunchsorted.app keeps the fetch first-party (and under MPP, Apple's proxy makes even that fetch non-identifying), but it still creates a server log line per Apple Mail open; if the policy is "no open measurement at all," use no remote fonts and accept the system stack.
- Fallback stacks that keep the voice: Familjen Grotesk and Karla are grotesque sans faces, so `font-family: 'Familjen Grotesk', -apple-system, 'Helvetica Neue', Arial, sans-serif` (headlines) and `'Karla', -apple-system, 'Helvetica Neue', Arial, sans-serif` (body) land on San Francisco in Apple Mail and Roboto/Arial in Gmail; IBM Plex Mono should fall to `Menlo, Consolas, 'Courier New', monospace`. (-apple-system support in Apple Mail is widely used in practice; I did not fetch a Can I Email page for it, so treat as practitioner convention.)
- Add `mso-font-alt` only if Outlook for Windows matters; at 3.48% of opens for Outlook overall (Sept 2026), it does not for this audience.

### Gaps
- Can I Email's `@font-face` page is dated December 2023; no 2025–26 retest was located. Gmail's position has not changed in any source found.
- No source found quantifies how many Apple Mail opens are MPP-proxied for fonts specifically (as distinct from images).

## 5. Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 applied to email; EMC and Litmus reports

### Takeaway
The Email Markup Consortium's 2025 report (443,585 emails) found 99.89% had serious or critical issues; the fixes are mechanical and cheap: `lang` and `dir` on `<html>` and body, `role="presentation"` on layout tables, a `<title>`, one `<h1>`, descriptive link text, alt text on every image, and 7:1 contrast (the brand already requires AAA). WCAG 2.2 adds 24px minimum targets (AA) and 80-character lines (AAA).

### Cited Findings
- EMC Email Accessibility Report 2025 (published October 13, 2025; emails collected May 2024–May 2025; Parcel's automated checker): 443,585 emails; 99.89% with "Serious" or "Critical" issues; only 21 emails (two brands) passed; Critical issues in 60.66%, Serious in 39.23%. Top issues: body content missing `dir` 98.14%; body missing `lang` 96.67%; layout tables lacking `role="presentation"`/`"none"` 86.24%; no level-one heading 76.78%; links without discernible text 72.04%; `<html>` missing `lang` 67.01%; insufficient color contrast 59.37%; images missing alt 51.42%; missing `<title>` 41.35%; non-descriptive link text 17.62%. Critical issues include VML elements without accessibility attributes, links inside VML unreachable by keyboard, and `role="button"` on anchors. Only one client (SFR Mail) supported all 20 tested accessibility features. — [EMC 2025](https://emailmarkup.org/en/reports/accessibility/2025/)
- EMC 2026 report (reported second-hand): 376,348 emails, 99.88% with serious/critical defects, eight passing emails from three brands. — [Harvard TagTeam feed item](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/2087/feed_items/18322019) (the report itself is presumably at emailmarkup.org/en/reports/accessibility/2026/, not fetched)
- WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.6 Contrast (Enhanced, AAA): text and images of text at least 7:1; large-scale text (at least 18 point, or 14 point bold; about 24px / 18.66px CSS) at least 4.5:1; exceptions for inactive components, decoration, logos, incidental text in photographs. — [W3C Understanding 1.4.6](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-enhanced.html)
- WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 (24x24 CSS px, AA) and SC 1.4.8 (80 characters, no justification, 1.5 leading) as cited in section 1. — [W3C 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html); [W3C 1.4.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/visual-presentation.html)
- Resend's own accessibility primer exists ("6 tips for accessible emails"); content not reviewed in this session. — [Resend blog](https://resend.com/blog/6-tips-for-accessible-emails)
- Litmus: "Never show a state by fading text or by color alone" is the project's rule (CLAUDE.md), and matches WCAG 1.4.1 Use of Color; styled alt text is unsupported in Outlook 2007–2019. — [Litmus styled alt text](https://www.litmus.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-styled-alt-text-in-email/)

### Inferences
- A checklist for the template: `<html lang="en" dir="ltr">`, `<title>` equal to the subject, `role="presentation"` on every layout `<table>`, one `<h1>` (the headline), real `<p>` text, a bulletproof button that is an `<a>` with padding (not `role="button"`), link text that names the destination ("Open this week's plan," not "Click here"), `alt` on the screenshot that says what it shows ("Tuesday's box: hummus, carrots, pita, an apple"), `alt=""` on spacers, left-aligned text, 16–17px body with `line-height: 1.5`, and the app's own AAA-checked token pairs for every text/background combination in both light and dark palettes.
- Since the project already has `npm run contrast`, extend it to the email palette (light, dark, and Gmail-inverted approximations) rather than adopting a second tool.

### Gaps
- No Litmus accessibility report with 2025–26 data was located in this session; EMC's reports are the primary dataset.
- Screen-reader behavior of Apple Mail iOS with VoiceOver for `role="presentation"` tables was not tested or sourced here.

## 6. Plain-text part: still recommended? what should it contain?

### Takeaway
Yes, send multipart/alternative with a deliberate text/plain part. Resend generates one from the HTML automatically unless you pass `text: ""`, but a hand-written one reads better on Apple Watch (which drops HTML to text), in notification previews, and for anyone who sets plain-text mode. The deliverability effect is widely asserted and weakly evidenced.

### Cited Findings
- Resend send API, `text` parameter: "If not provided, the HTML will be used to generate a plain text version. You can opt out of this behavior by setting value to an empty string." Also exposes `headers`, `tags`, `reply_to`, `scheduled_at`, attachments up to 40 MB per email. — [Resend API: send email](https://resend.com/docs/api-reference/emails/send-email)
- Apple Watch "only displays the plain text portion of an email, ignoring things like remote images"; the iPhone converts HTML to rich text before sending to the Watch; a `text/watch-html` MIME part is shown instead when the HTML part contains a remote image (ordering: text/plain, text/watch-html, text/html last). Discovered 2015 by a Litmus user; still the only documented behavior. — [MacTrast 2015](https://www.mactrast.com/2015/05/html-handler-quirk-allows-email-content-visible-only-on-apple-watch/amp/); [FreshInbox](https://freshinbox.com/blog/?p=1735); [Bird](https://bird.com/blog/email-on-apple-watch)
- Deliverability-focused Q&A (secondary, 2025): recommend multipart/alternative with "a useful plain text part"; direct deliverability impact described as "minimal"; text/plain still matters for watch notifications, terminal clients, email-to-SMS gateways, ticketing systems, machine processing, and user-selected plain-text mode. — [Suped: should I still send multipart/alternative](https://suped.com/email-deliverability/answers/should-i-still-send-multipartalternative-emails); [Suped: when plain text is needed](https://www.suped.com/email-deliverability/answers/when-are-plain-text-emails-needed-and-which-devices-still-use-them)

### Inferences
- Content of the text part: the same headline, one or two sentences, the single URL written out in full (not a tracking redirect, so it opens the app via universal link when tapped from Mail), the household/support address, and the unsubscribe URL spelled out when the email is a lifecycle message. Keep lines under 78 characters per RFC 5322 convention and mirror the HTML so spam filters do not see mismatched parts (mismatch as a spam signal is folklore; the mirrored content is good practice regardless).
- Auto-generated text from a table-based HTML often carries stray spaces, "View in browser" junk, and image alt text out of order; writing `text` by hand costs one string per template.
- `text/watch-html` is a 2015 quirk; do not build for it, but the plain-text part covers the Watch case.

### Gaps
- No 2024–26 primary data from a mailbox provider or Litmus/Validity quantifying the deliverability effect of omitting text/plain.
- Whether Apple Watch in watchOS 10–26 still prefers text/plain was not re-verified by a 2025+ source.

## 7. Deliverability for a low-volume Resend sender: SPF, DKIM, DMARC, subdomain, List-Unsubscribe/RFC 8058, Gmail and Yahoo rules, unsubscribe in transactional mail

### Takeaway
Below 5,000 messages a day to Gmail you are an "all senders" sender: SPF or DKIM, a PTR record, TLS, RFC 5322 formatting, spam rate under 0.3% (aim under 0.1%). Do the bulk-sender set anyway because it is cheap: SPF and DKIM both aligned to the From domain, DMARC `p=none` with `rua`, and RFC 8058 one-click headers on every lifecycle (non-transactional) email. Resend does not add List-Unsubscribe to API sends; you add it in `headers`. Transactional mail (magic links, receipts) is exempt from one-click under Google's FAQ and Resend's guidance; trial reminders and onboarding nudges are not transactional and should carry both the header and a visible link. Send from a subdomain (mail.lunchsorted.app), as Resend recommends.

### Cited Findings
- Google Email sender guidelines (effective February 1, 2024): all senders must "Set up SPF or DKIM email authentication for your sending domains," have valid forward and reverse DNS (PTR), "Use a TLS connection for transmitting email," comply with RFC 5322, keep spam rates in Postmaster Tools low (below 0.3%; Google's text names 0.10% as the target and 0.30% as the ceiling), and not impersonate Gmail in From. Bulk senders (5,000+/day to personal Gmail) must additionally "Set up SPF and DKIM," "Set up DMARC" (policy may be `none`), align the From domain with SPF or DKIM, support one-click unsubscribe for "Marketing messages and subscribed messages" via `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and include a clearly visible unsubscribe link in the body. — [Google sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)
- Google FAQ: "One-click unsubscribe is required only for marketing and promotional messages. Transactional messages are excluded from this requirement" (examples: password resets, reservation confirmations, form submission confirmations). The 5,000 count sums all subdomains of one primary domain. The guidelines "apply only when sending email to personal Gmail accounts," not Google Workspace recipients. Non-compliance yields temporary/permanent failure codes (4.7.23, 4.7.27, 4.7.29–32, 5.7.25, 5.7.27, 5.7.29, 5.7.30) or spam foldering; June 2024: senders over 0.3% spam lose mitigation eligibility; November 2025: Gmail "is ramping up its enforcement on non-compliant traffic." — [Google sender FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414)
- Yahoo sender requirements (enforced from February 2024): all senders "Implement SPF or DKIM at a minimum," keep spam rate below 0.3%; bulk senders "Implement both SPF & DKIM," "Publish a valid DMARC policy with at least p=none," and "Implement a functioning list-unsubscribe header, which supports one-click unsubscribe," with "The Post (RFC 8058) method... highly recommended." The page does not spell out a transactional exemption. — [Yahoo sender best practices](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/)
- RFC 8058: "The List-Unsubscribe header field MUST contain one HTTPS URI"; "The List-Unsubscribe-Post header MUST contain the single key/value pair 'List-Unsubscribe=One-Click'"; POST body "SHOULD be sent as 'multipart/form-data' or MAY be sent as 'application/x-www-form-urlencoded'"; "The mail sender MUST NOT return an HTTPS redirect"; "The List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers MUST be covered by the signature and included in the 'h=' tag of a valid DKIM-Signature header field"; "The URI SHOULD include an opaque identifier or another hard-to-forge component." — [RFC 8058](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8058)
- Resend: "Resend does not automatically add List-Unsubscribe headers to transactional emails"; add `headers: { 'List-Unsubscribe': '<https://example.com/unsubscribe>', 'List-Unsubscribe-Post': 'List-Unsubscribe=One-Click' }` on `resend.emails.send()`; the endpoint must answer POST with a blank 200 or 202 and serve the normal page on GET; "Ensure that users stop receiving email within 48 hours of this request." — [Resend: add unsubscribe to transactional emails](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/add-unsubscribe-to-transactional-emails)
- Resend knowledge base: "Transactional emails are generally exempt from including an unsubscribe link"; relationship-building mail should include one; Broadcasts/Automations manage unsubscribes automatically via the Unsubscribe Footer or `{{{RESEND_UNSUBSCRIBE_URL}}}`. — [Resend: should I add an unsubscribe link](https://resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/should-i-add-an-unsubscribe-link)
- Resend domains: recommends "sending your emails from one or more subdomains (e.g., updates.example.com) instead of your root domain" for reputation isolation; custom Return-Path available; open/click tracking can be enabled for newsletters while "remaining disabled for transactional emails like password resets." — [Resend: domains](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/domains/introduction)
- Resend DMARC: start with `v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarcreports@example.com;` "To ensure you don't accidentally introduce breaking changes"; the `rua` address may be on another domain; "An email must pass either SPF or DKIM checks (but not necessarily both) to achieve DMARC compliance." — [Resend: DMARC](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/domains/dmarc)

### Inferences
- Lunch Sorted's mail splits into: transactional (magic link, receipt, invite, delete confirmation) with no unsubscribe required, and lifecycle (first-week nudges, `cron-trial.js` reminders, what's-new) which is "subscribed" mail under Google's definition; give the latter RFC 8058 headers, a visible unsubscribe link, and a one-click POST endpoint (a Netlify function that flips a household/member flag and returns 200 with an empty body, no redirect). Suppress within 48 hours means immediately in practice.
- The 5,000/day threshold is counted per primary domain, so mail.lunchsorted.app and lunchsorted.app share a bucket; at current volumes the program is nowhere near it, but enforcement codes in November 2025 suggest Gmail now applies the baseline rules to everyone, so authenticate fully regardless.
- Use a Resend custom Return-Path on the sending subdomain so SPF aligns as well as DKIM; then DMARC passes on both.

### Gaps
- Whether Resend Broadcasts add `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (one-click) automatically, as opposed to the footer link only, could not be confirmed: the "handle unsubscribes" page returned 404 and the Broadcasts intro does not say. Check Resend's current docs before relying on Broadcasts for lifecycle mail.
- Whether Resend's DKIM `h=` tag covers the `List-Unsubscribe` headers (an RFC 8058 MUST) is undocumented; send a test to a Gmail account and read the "Show original" DKIM signature.
- Google's exact wording of the 0.10%/0.30% spam-rate line was summarized by the fetch, not quoted; verify the quote on the page.

## 8. Measurement without tracking the person: MPP, click tracking and the "nothing leaves the phone" policy, Resend's toggles, a privacy-respecting plan

### Takeaway
Apple's MPP loads remote content through Apple's proxy and hides the IP, so for roughly three-quarters of this audience an open pixel reports an "open" the moment the message arrives, not when a parent reads it; Litmus puts MPP at 55–60% of all opens. Resend's open and click tracking are off by default and per-domain; click tracking rewrites every link through a tracking subdomain, which both creates per-person click records at Resend and breaks iOS universal links. Measure outcomes instead: a per-template counter incremented server-side when the deep link resolves (no recipient identifier), or "came back within 24h" computed from data the server already holds.

### Cited Findings
- Apple: Mail Privacy Protection "hides your IP address so senders can't create a profile with your other online activity or see where you are" and senders "can't tell if you've opened their email." — [Apple privacy features](https://www.apple.com/privacy/features/)
- Litmus: Apple is 76.56% of opens (September 2026) and "Apple's MPP now impact roughly 55-60% of all email opens"; MPP opens do not pass client version or platform. — [Litmus market share](https://www.litmus.com/email-client-market-share)
- Median open rate across 3.6 million 2025 campaigns was 43.46% "but that number now includes millions of opens nobody actually made" because MPP "pre-fetches every tracking pixel the instant a message lands." (Secondary roundup.) — [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/open-rate-statistics)
- Resend tracking: "Open and click tracking is disabled by default for all domains." Open tracking inserts "a 1x1 pixel transparent GIF image... in each email" with a unique identifier. Click tracking: "Resend modifies each link in the body of the HTML email to point to your tracking subdomain," records the click, then redirects; a custom tracking subdomain (CNAME, same root as the sending domain) is required; tracking is configured per domain, not per email. Resend itself warns that some providers "open the email prior to delivering to scan for malware or to protect user privacy," which "can trigger an open event without the recipient reading your email." — [Resend: tracking](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/domains/tracking); [Resend blog: open and click tracking](https://resend.com/blog/open-and-click-tracking); [Resend blog: custom tracking domain](https://resend.com/blog/introducing-custom-tracking-domain); [Resend changelog: update tracking via API](https://resend.com/changelog/update-click-open-tracking-via-api)
- Resend webhook events include email.sent, delivered, delivery_delayed, bounced, failed, opened, clicked, complained, received, scheduled, suppressed, plus domain/contact/suppression/topic events. — [Resend: webhook event types](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/webhooks/event-types)
- Resend tags: "up to 75 tags per email," ASCII letters, numbers, underscores or dashes; "After the email is sent, the tag is included in the webhook event." — [Resend: tags](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/tags)
- Tracking redirects break universal links (see section 9): wrapped links open Safari, not the app. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 757122](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/757122)

### Inferences
- An open pixel is both useless (MPP) and a per-person remote fetch; leave Resend open tracking off. Leave click tracking off too: it writes a per-recipient click event (with whatever request metadata Resend keeps) to a third party, and it defeats the deep link.
- A plan that fits the policy: (1) each template links to a first-party URL carrying a template token, e.g. `https://lunchsorted.app/app/?from=week1-tue`, with no recipient identifier; (2) a Netlify function or edge redirect increments a per-template, per-day counter (one integer row per template-day) and strips the parameter before the app sees it; (3) "returned within 24h of the email" is computed server-side from timestamps the household document already carries (last sync, `updatedAt`) joined to the send log by household id, aggregated to counts, with no new per-person table; (4) delivery health (bounces, complaints, suppressions) comes from Resend webhooks, which carry no reading behavior; tags (`template=week1-tue`) let those webhook events be grouped without a customer id in the tag. This gives delivered, clicked-through (by template), and returned-in-24h rates without any per-recipient behavioral record outside what the product already stores.
- Note that counting a deep-link hit is a click count, not a person count; MPP and Gmail do not pre-fetch links, so the counter is clean of proxy noise (there is no source that any major client pre-fetches link targets; Resend's note concerns opens).
- If a per-household "has returned" flag is ever needed, it should live in the household row the server already has and be derived, not logged as an event.

### Gaps
- Resend's `email.clicked`/`email.opened` webhook payload fields (IP address, user agent, link) were not retrieved; the event-types page summary omitted payload schemas. Check the payload reference before deciding whether even delivery webhooks carry data you would rather not receive.
- No primary Apple figure for MPP adoption; Litmus's 55–60% is the best available (2025–26).
- No source addresses whether Resend's redirect preserves universal links when the tracking subdomain is itself listed in the app's associated domains (in principle iOS evaluates the first URL tapped, so listing `links.lunchsorted.app` in `applinks` and serving an AASA there could make the native app open and perform the redirect in-app; untested, inference only).

## 9. Deep links from email into the PWA and the Capacitor iOS app: universal links, fallbacks, Gmail wrapping, Mail opening Safari rather than the home-screen PWA

### Takeaway
With the Capacitor app installed, a direct `https://lunchsorted.app/...` link tapped in Apple Mail opens the app via universal links if the AASA lists the path; the same link in the Gmail app may open Chrome or an in-app browser and land on the web app instead; any tracking redirect defeats universal links everywhere. For the home-screen PWA there is no equivalent: iOS routes links from Mail to Safari (sometimes with an "open in web app" banner), so the web fallback must work signed-in or re-authenticate quickly.

### Cited Findings
- Capacitor deep links guide: add the Associated Domains capability with `applinks:yourdomain.com`; host `https://yourdomain.com/.well-known/apple-app-site-association` (HTTPS, no extension) containing `{"applinks":{"apps":[],"details":[{"appID":"TEAMID.BUNDLEID","paths":["*"]}]}}`; route with `App.addListener('appUrlOpen', ...)`; validate with Apple's app search validation tool; no App Store release needed to test on device. — [Capacitor deep links](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/guides/deep-links)
- Wrapped links: a SendGrid-wrapped tracking URL that redirects to ebay.com opens Safari, while the direct ebay.com link opens the eBay app; iOS evaluates the first URL tapped, not the redirect target; a January 2025 reply reports the same failure specifically from the Gmail app while the native Mail app worked for that poster; an Apple engineer suggested `curl -v` on the link to inspect the chain, with no further resolution. — [Apple Developer Forums 757122](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/757122)
- Customer.io documents the same: tracked links "redirect through the tracking service, so iOS won't automatically open your app when people tap the link"; the landing page must resolve the deep link. — [Customer.io: universal links](https://docs.customer.io/journeys/universal-links)
- Gmail iOS app browser setting: Gmail opens links in Chrome if installed, with Settings > Google Apps offering Chrome or Safari and an "Ask me which app to use every time" switch (older write-ups, 2015–2020; the setting still exists). — [AddictiveTips](https://www.addictivetips.com/ios/select-browser-gmail-opens-links-in-iphone); [iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/how-to-open-links-in-chrome-on-iphone-ipad/)
- Gmail rewrites URLs in message bodies through `https://www.google.com/url?q=...&source=gmail&ust=...` for link checking (documented for Gmail-composed/API-created mail; Gmail web also routes outbound clicks through this redirect). — [Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/tag/g-suite/); [Cursor forum report](https://forum.cursor.com/t/grok-bot-gmail-connector-user-gmail-rewrites-every-link-into-google-com-url-redirects-on-create-draft-and-send-message/173321/11)
- Home-screen web apps on iOS: external links in a home-screen web app open an in-app webview; links to the same domain from other apps may open in the web app's window, but behavior is inconsistent; a link sent via iMessage "on some devices... opens in Safari with a banner offering to open the web app, while on other devices it opens in Safari but no option to open in the web app appears" (December 2023). — [WebKit bug 247270](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=247270); [Adactio, "Stuck in the dock"](https://adactio.com/journal/20716); [TidBITS Talk](https://talk.tidbits.com/t/ipad-how-to-open-link-in-web-app-not-safari/33552)
- Forum reports through iOS 18.3 still describe links opening in Safari rather than the installed PWA, forcing re-login. — [Glide community](https://community.glideapps.com/t/open-url-from-other-app-in-pwa/59048)

### Inferences
- For Lunch Sorted: list only app-routable paths in the AASA `paths` (for example `/app/*`), and exclude `/.netlify/functions/*` and the magic-link endpoint so a sign-in link completes in Safari as the auth flow expects; the `appUrlOpen` handler should map `/app/?from=...&screen=pack` to the Pack view. Keep the exact same URL in the plain-text part.
- Never wrap the deep link in a tracking redirect; if a per-template count is wanted, let the destination itself count (section 8) rather than a hop before it. If Resend click tracking is ever enabled for a different domain's broadcasts, keep lifecycle mail on a domain where it stays off, since the setting is per domain.
- Expect three landing paths and make all three good: native app (Apple Mail tap, direct link); web app in Safari or Chrome (Gmail app, PWA users, no app installed) which should recognise the session cookie or offer the magic link in one tap; and Safari with the "Open in web app" banner, which the parent may or may not see.
- A user who once chose "open in Safari" from the universal-link banner has told iOS to prefer Safari for that domain until they choose the app again from the banner; this is Apple's long-documented behavior and worth a line in help copy, but I did not re-fetch Apple's page for it in this session (treat as known practitioner behavior, verify against Apple's "Supporting universal links" documentation).

### Gaps
- No primary Apple documentation was fetched this session for the universal-link requirements beyond the Capacitor summary (the Apple "Supporting associated domains" page should be cited directly in the implementation ticket).
- The Gmail iOS app's default link target in 2025–26 (in-app browser vs. Chrome vs. Safari) rests on older write-ups; test on a device.
- No source confirms whether Gmail's `google.com/url` wrapper is applied to links in received third-party HTML mail on the iOS app (as opposed to Gmail web and Gmail-composed mail); the Apple forum report of universal links failing from the Gmail app is consistent with an intermediate hop or an in-app browser but does not prove which.
- Nothing found on Safari's "Open in web app" banner behavior for PWAs in iOS 18–26 from Apple itself; the behavior is documented only by practitioners.
