# Transactional and account emails in consumer food, meal-planning, grocery and family-organizer apps (and admired phone-first subscription apps)

Scope note for the writer: real captured emails from the named food/family apps are thin on the public galleries. Really Good Emails' brand pages returned 404 for every brand tried (Headspace, Instacart, HelloFresh, Duolingo), Milled is behind a bot check, and two Medium/UX Collective teardowns were 403. The richest primary source turned out to be Sequenzy (sequenzy.com), which captures real signup-flow emails with dates (most captures Sep–Oct 2026) and publishes aggregate statistics per category. Word counts below are the fetch tool's own counts of the quoted body and are marked "est." throughout.

## Key question 1: Sign-in / magic-link / one-time-code emails — how long, how the code is shown, link vs code, expiry language

### Takeaway
The admired code emails are 20–45 words of body: a one-line headline, the code set alone on its own line, one button for the link, one "ignore this if you didn't ask" line, and nothing else. Across 168 captured login-code emails, 64% are plain text, 60% have a single button, and the median subject is about 25 characters — frequently the code itself in the subject. Expiry wording, when present, is a single sentence ("This code will expire in 10 minutes. It can only be used once."); several well-known senders (Notion, Substack, Stripe's verify email) carry no expiry sentence at all.

### Cited Findings
- Aggregate over 168 magic-link / login-code emails from 123 brands: 64% plain-text formatting, 60% a single call-to-action button, only 5% personalised, median subject about 25 characters, fewer than 1% use emoji; the site's stated pattern is "Large, spaced digits near the top of the email, and ideally in the subject line", plus showing device/location details and keeping subjects unique so threads don't collapse in the inbox — [Sequenzy: SaaS magic link and login code examples](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/saas-magic-link)

### Inferences
- The two dominant shapes are (a) code-only, no button (Instacart, Slack) and (b) code + one "sign in" button (Notion, Linear). Nobody captured explains link-versus-code in more than one sentence; Notion's "You can sign up by entering the code ... or simply by clicking the magic link" is the model.
- Putting the code in the subject line (Instacart's "005650 is your ...") is now common enough to be a stated pattern; it lets iOS/Mail autofill and lets a parent read it from the notification without opening the email.
- Expiry sentences are short and concrete ("10 minutes", "once") or absent; nobody writes a paragraph about security. Instacart's phishing paragraph is the outlier and is what pushes it past 100 words.

### Gaps
- No verbatim capture of Linear's, Slack's genuine, Apple's or Stripe Dashboard's code email body was reachable (galleries 404/403; the one Slack specimen has a suspicious sender).
- Nothing at all captured for Mealime, Paprika, Plan to Eat, Samsung Food, Yummly, eMeals, Cozi, AnyList or OurGroceries sign-in emails; Milled was behind a bot check and the apps' help centers describe flows, not email text.

## Key question 2: Welcome emails — one CTA or several, screenshot/illustration, checklist, founder note, length

### Takeaway
Across 1,757 captured welcome emails only 29% have a single CTA and 24% are plain text, but the admired consumer examples sit at the short end: Duolingo's welcome introduces the owl and sets one goal, Linear's offers three links, Superhuman's is ~105 words with one button, Headspace's is the long one (~165 words, two primary buttons and three "learn more" links). Several big apps (Instacart, Substack, Stripe) send no welcome at signup at all — only the verification email — and Calm's "welcome" is a discount offer.

### Cited Findings
- Aggregate over 1,757 welcome emails from 1,419 brands: 31% personalised, 29% single CTA, 24% plain text, 19% carry a discount code (the same page elsewhere says 16%), 14% in a founder's voice, median subject 29–30 characters, 89% sent on Day 0, emoji in about 20% of subjects; "many follow the same shape: 'Welcome to' followed by the product name" — [Sequenzy: welcome email examples](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/welcome)
- **Duolingo**, subject "Welcome to Duolingo! Here are 2 important tips." preheader "A guaranteed path to learning English more effectively." (Sep 29 2026, Day 0), followed by activation nudges on Days 1, 2, 4, 5 ("👀 Hmm. Interesting." / "No lesson?"; "💔 Don't go breaking my heart"; "Your English skills are getting moldy 🤢"; "You know what I want") and a Day 7 "Your last reminder from me" / "At least for now" — [Sequenzy: Duolingo](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/brands/duolingo). Loops' read of the same email: "short and sweet", introduces the mascot, sets one concrete goal ("3 minutes a day for a week"), and tells the user a 7-day streak is the likeliest path to a habit — [Loops: welcome email examples](https://loops.so/examples/welcome-email-examples)
- **Headspace**, subject "Welcome to Headspace", preheader "We're happy you're here" (Sep 25 2026, via SendGrid, ~5pm UTC). Body opens "Discover more good days with Headspace" / "Congrats on taking the first step towards a happier and healthier you." / "Headspace is here for you anytime you need a moment to take care of your mind — it only takes a few minutes a day." - Shapes Loops singles out: Dub (founder greeting + three bullets of core features), Descript (five-step checklist + community link), todays.design ("dead simple" single sentence and one button), founder-led openers from Plot and June; its rules: subject "specific and action-tied" e.g. "Welcome to [Product], let's get you set up", "One dominant CTA outperforms multiple competing buttons", series Day 0 welcome / Day 1 setup nudge if needed / Day 3 first-win example — [Loops](https://loops.so/examples/welcome-email-examples)

### Inferences
- The welcome emails people cite as good share three traits: a subject that promises something concrete ("Here are 2 important tips", "let's get you set up"), one named first action, and under ~110 words. Headspace shows what happens when a welcome tries to do four things: it doubles in length and grows five links.
- Feature education is routinely split out of the welcome into a Day 1–7 series of one-topic emails (Notion, Linear, Duolingo), each with a subject that is the tip itself. That matches the brief's three first-week education emails.
- Founder voice is a minority (14%) but recognised pattern; it is used as the opener's device, not as a long letter.

### Gaps
- No verbatim welcome bodies for Mealime, Paprika, Plan to Eat, eMeals, Cozi, AnyList, HelloFresh or Instacart were reachable. Plan to Eat's marketing pages confirm founder Clint Bounds and a 30-day no-card trial, but not its email copy — [Plan to Eat](https://www.plantoeat.com/ref/8128388d6b).
- The UX Collective "20 days of Duolingo emails" teardown (which quotes bodies) returned 403 and could not be read.

## Key question 3: Trial-ending and trial-ended emails — how far ahead, what is above the fold, price, lost vs kept

### Takeaway
The captured norm is a reminder around 2–3 days before the end (median Day 10 of a 14-day trial), a subject that states the date or the days left, a single button in 58–63% of cases, and almost never a discount (2 of 212). The best-regarded ones name what the person built or what they will lose/keep ("Here is what you'll lose", "keep your free account!", "What to expect"), and the trial-ended email restates what still works on the free tier. Outliers: Noom converts a paid trial without a separate notice (and has been sued over it); Plan to Eat never auto-charges, so its trial-ended email can only be an invitation.

### Cited Findings
- Aggregate over 212 trial-ending emails: median subject 38 characters, 23% plain text, single CTA 58–63%, typical send Day 10 after signup, emoji 11–12%, 53% personalised, discounts in only 2 of 212; "Most emails avoid discounts, focusing instead on value recapitulation" — [Sequenzy: trial-ending examples](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/trial-ending)
- Subject lines from that set: "⏳ [First name] Your trial is ending, keep your free account!" (Sendbird, Day 28); "Trial ending soon for [First name]'s workspace" (Count, Day 11); "Your Yesware free trial expires in 48 hours" (Day 11); "Your Pro trial ends in 2 days. Here is what you'll lose." (Novu, Day 12, button **"Upgrade in a minute"**, offers to "Keep your Inbox, higher limits, and Pro features"); Depot sends "a short personal note that offers help with internal approval or more trial time" — [Sequenzy: trial-ending examples](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/trial-ending); [Sequenzy: Novu sequence](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/brands/novu/sequences/onboarding)
- **Sentry**: Day 10 "Trial ends October 15: What to expect" — "Explains what happens when the Business trial ends and compares continuing on a paid plan with automatically moving to the free plan", with a plan-selection button; Day 13 "Your free 14-day Business trial has ended" (describes the limited free plan, includes a usage summary) and a second Day 13 email "You're still getting 5,000 free Session Replays" (a kept benefit for three months) — [Sequenzy: Sentry sequence](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/brands/sentry/sequences/onboarding)
- **Kit (ConvertKit)**: Day 6 "Your trial is half over: Do this next" ("Add a payment card before your trial ends to keep access to powerful features"); Day 11 "48 hours left to lock in your progress"; Day 12 "Keep your momentum going"; Day 12 "Your free trial has expired" — [Sequenzy: Kit sequence](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/brands/kit-kit/sequences/onboarding)
- **Sendbird**: Day 18 "2 must-try features before your trial ends" (5 days out); Day 28 "⏳ [First name] Your trial is ending, keep your free account!" — "It's the end of the trial period, but this doesn't have to be the end", pointing to a free developer plan — [Sequenzy: Sendbird sequence](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/brands/sendbird/sequences/onboarding)
- Other captured subjects: "[Fly.io] Your trial expires in 3 days – Avoid organization suspension" and "... expires tomorrow ..."; "You have 7 days left on your free trial, [First name]" (Endorsal, Day 6); "Your Scheduling Trial Is Expiring Soon" (Acuity, two days out) — [Sequenzy blog: SaaS email sequence examples](https://www.sequenzy.com/blog/saas-email-sequence-examples); [Sequenzy: Endorsal](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/brands/endorsal/emails/you-have-7-days-left-on-your-free-trial-first-name)
- Sequenzy's trial-ending playbook (Jan 27 2026): a 3-day warning "emphasizing what users built", a final-day email, a loss-aversion variant "focusing on access loss", a soft-urgency variant "offering multiple options"; strong emails "reference specific work the user did" rather than generic copy — [Sequenzy blog: trial conversion emails](https://www.sequenzy.com/blog/trial-conversion-email-examples)
- **Noom** (outlier): its terms let it charge "immediately after a free trial ends without providing a separate notice that the free trial is about to end" — [ConductAtlas: Noom terms provision](https://conductatlas.com/platform/noom/noom-terms-of-service/free-trial-converts-to-paid-without-separate-notice/); BBB complaints describe people billed "for several months upfront upon the free trial's end" and a class action alleging the "no strings attached" trial converted to auto-renewal without awareness — [KSAT/BBB report](https://ksat.com/news/local/2020/08/19/consumers-are-losing-more-than-weight-with-noom-health-app-better-business-bureau-says); [CT Watchdog](https://ctwatchdog.com/finance/scams-finance/noom-facing-100-million-lawsuit-over-cheating-its-customers)
- **Plan to Eat** (outlier the other way): "a 30-day extended trial completely free ... no credit card is required when you sign up and Plan to Eat does not automatically charge for a subscription" — [Plan to Eat help: cancel trial or subscription](https://learn.plantoeat.com/doc/how-do-i-cancel-my-30-day-trial-or-subscription)
- **App Store trials** (Headspace, Calm and every iOS subscription): the purchase sheet itself shows "the trial length, subscription start date, subscription price, subscription length, and a message stating to avoid the subscription charge you must cancel a day before the trial period ends" — [Michael Tsai blog, App Store trial opt-out](https://mjtsai.com/blog/?p=29407); Tom's Guide credits Apple with making "very clear that your trial's about to end through email reminders" for Apple's own services — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/uk/opinion/apple-tv-plus-has-one-great-feature-i-wish-other-streaming-services-would-copy)
- **Calm**'s trial is cancelled through the App Store / Play / web account rather than by email, and the Aug 2023 teardown of Calm's first 135 days found no trial-ending email at all — only offers — [Calm support: cancel free trial](https://support.calm.com/hc/en-us/articles/360003084793-Cancel-Calm-Free-Trial-Website-App-Instructions); [Growth Snippets](https://growthsnippets.substack.com/p/every-email-calm-sent-me-after-i)
- Really Good Emails' Headspace captures are all offers ("Don't let 20% off pass you by", "Get 20% off a year of Headspace", "Your guide to a happier new year" with a 30-day free trial); none is a trial-ending notice — [RGE text view](https://reallygoodemails.com/emails/dont-let-20-off-pass-you-by/text); [RGE text view](https://reallygoodemails.com/emails/your-guide-to-a-happier-new-year/text)

### Inferences
- Phone-first consumer apps billed through the App Store mostly do not write their own trial-ending email; the stores do the notice and the apps send offers. The ones that do write their own (web-billed SaaS) converge on: date in the subject, two to three days ahead, one button, a "what you keep / what you lose" list, and a separate trial-ended email that restates what the free tier still does.
- Price appears on the store sheet, not in the captured emails; the Sequenzy stats (discount in 2 of 212) suggest price-talk in the reminder is the exception, and the "lose/keep" list is the norm.
- For Lunch Sorted (three free weeks, not an entitlement; nothing deleted when it ends) the Sendbird/Sentry "keep your free account" / "What to expect" framing is the closer precedent than any charge warning.

### Gaps
- No full bodies of a consumer-app trial-ending or trial-ended email were reachable; Sequenzy's sequence pages give subjects, days and one-line summaries only.
- Apple's own "free trial ending" email text (sent by Apple for App Store subscriptions) was not found; Apple's developer forum thread 759883 covers server notifications only — [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/759883)
- Nothing found for eMeals, Mealime, Paprika, Yummly or Samsung Food trial emails.

## Key question 4: "You were invited to share a family / household" emails — Cozi, AnyList, OurGroceries, Apple Family Sharing, 1Password Families, Google Family Link

### Takeaway
Household-invite emails are short and name the inviter in the subject ("[Name] wants you to join her family group"; "Join [Account] on 1Password"), carry one button ("Accept invitation", a "join" button), state what the joiner will be able to see, and expire (Google: two weeks). Two of the grocery apps don't send an invite email to existing users at all — AnyList pushes a notification and OurGroceries just has both people type the same email — and Cozi's "invite" is really an email-verification email.

### Cited Findings
- **Google family group**: subject shape "[Name] wants you to join her/his family group"; body "You can join [Family member's name]'s family group to connect with your family on Google and share the apps and services that are right for you."; link **"Accept invitation"**; the email tells joiners that group members can see each other's name, email and profile picture — [MIT mailing-list copy of the email, June 2023](https://mailman.mit.edu/pipermail/mitreid-connect/2023-June/000718.html) (a forwarded copy; wording relayed by the search summary). Google's help: "If someone invites you to join their family, you'll get an email or text message. Follow the instructions in the invitation to join the family group." and "Invitations to join a family expire after 2 weeks." — [Google Families help](https://support.google.com/families/answer/6317858)
- **1Password**: "To join a family account, open the invitation email and select the join button, then follow the onscreen instructions." — [1Password support: Join a family](https://support.1password.com/join-family/); invitation emails come from accounts@1password.com with a subject of the form "Join [Account name] on 1Password" — [UW-Madison KB on 1Password](https://kb.wisc.edu/security/147715); a community thread complains that guest invites are "confusingly received as 'Family' invites" — [AgileBits community thread](https://discussions.agilebits.com/discussion/comment/559001)
- **Apple Family Sharing**: the organizer "can send an invitation via Messages or email"; the invited person accepts from the message or in Settings — [Apple support: set up Family Sharing](https://support.apple.com/ht201088); no email body text is published
- **Cozi**: adding a family member with an email address makes Cozi "send your family member an email to verify the email address you added"; the recipient taps a "verify email" button (blog dated Jan 10 2024) — [Cozi blog: add a family member](https://www.cozi.com/blog/how-to-add-family-to-cozi/); Cozi's FAQ adds that when the shared password is changed "other members of the account will be notified by email", and that daily/weekly agenda emails and reminder emails exist — [Cozi FAQ](https://www.cozi.com/faq/)
- **AnyList**: "If the person already has an AnyList account, they'll receive a push notification and the list will be shared. If they don't have an AnyList account, they'll receive an email with a link to download the app. Once they register for an account using the same email address where they received the invitation, the list will be shared." — [AnyList help: Share a list](https://help.anylist.com/articles/share-list/)
- **OurGroceries**: sharing works "by simply entering the same email address into each copy of the OurGroceries app"; no invitation email is described — [OurGroceries user guide](https://ourgroceries.com/user-guide)
- **Notion "Join your team on Notion"** (Sep 26 2026, Day 1) as the SaaS comparator: "📬 Forward this email to your teammates" / "Join your team on Notion" / "You have been invited by [First name] [First name] to [First name] [First name]'s Space." / "Click the link below to join!" / a bare app.notion.com link / one-line footer; about 41 words est. — [Sequenzy: Notion team invite](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/brands/notion/emails/join-your-team-on-notion)

### Inferences
- The invite email's job is done in one sentence that names the inviter and the thing being shared, plus one button; the consumer ones add a privacy line (what the joiner will see) and an expiry.
- Because AnyList and OurGroceries skip email for existing users, a parent inviting a partner may expect the invite to arrive as a notification, not a message; the email matters most when the second parent has no account yet — which is AnyList's only email case ("a link to download the app").

## Key question 5: What a concise, well-regarded one-time-code email looks like (Slack, Notion, Linear, Apple, Stripe, Substack) — plus receipts, payment failed, account deleted

### Takeaway
The concise model is Notion's: a two-word headline, the code alone on a line, one button, one "ignore if not you" sentence, a one-line footer — about 40 words. Receipts and payment-failed emails from the big processors and meal kits answer three questions (what happened, what to do, what happens next) and carry a deadline in bold; account-deleted messaging is "sad to see you go" plus a restore window, and the one deletion-adjacent transactional line captured is Duolingo's "Didn't create an account? Click here to remove this email address."

### Cited Findings
- Notion's login-code and signup-code emails, quoted in full under Q1 (about 42 and 44 words est., one button "Sign in with Magic Link", "Never share this code with anyone. ## Key question 6: Recurring patterns and outliers across the catalogue (for the eight Lunch Sorted emails)

### Takeaway
Across categories the admired emails are short lines, not paragraphs: a subject that already says the thing, one button whose label is the action, and the body as two to five lines. Length grows only when an email tries to do two jobs (Headspace welcome) or adds defensive prose (Instacart). The outliers worth knowing are the apps that send nothing (no welcome at all; no trial notice), not apps that send more.

### Cited Findings
- Category medians from Sequenzy's captures: login-code subject ~25 chars, 64% plain text, 60% single CTA; welcome subject ~29 chars, 24% plain text, 29% single CTA; trial-ending subject 38 chars, 23% plain text, 58–63% single CTA, discounts 2 of 212 — [magic link](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/saas-magic-link); [welcome](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/welcome); [trial ending](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/trial-ending)
- Subjects that state the content rather than announce the email: "Welcome to Duolingo! Here are 2 important tips.", "Trial ends October 15: What to expect", "Your Pro trial ends in 2 days. Here is what you'll lose.", "005650 is your Instacart verification code", "[Name] wants you to join her family group" — sources as cited above
- Button labels captured: "Sign in with Magic Link" (Notion), "Confirm your email" (Substack), "Verify email" (Stripe), "Start a free trial" (Headspace), "Explore your Superhuman suite", "Upgrade in a minute" (Novu), "Accept invitation" (Google), "View Offer" (Calm), "Get Started" (Noom per Mailtrap) — sources as cited above
- Short-line body style: Notion code email (five lines), Substack (three lines), Duolingo verify (two sentences + opt-out), Slack welcome "emoji bullet points for easy scanning", Dub "3 bullet points", Descript "5-step checklist" — sources as cited above
- Outliers: Instacart's 102-word code email because of a phishing paragraph; Headspace's 165-word welcome with five links; Calm's discount-as-welcome; Noom's no-notice trial conversion; Instacart/Substack/Stripe sending no welcome at all; AnyList/OurGroceries sending no invite email to existing users — sources as cited above
- One-goal rule from a transactional-email provider: magic-link emails "have only one goal" and should avoid "too much information or competing calls to action" — [Postmark](https://postmarkapp.com/blog/magic-links); Loops: "One dominant CTA outperforms multiple competing buttons" — [Loops](https://loops.so/examples/welcome-email-examples)

### Inferences
- A rewrite that reads as human in this genre is less about voice than about shape: subject = the fact, first line = the fact again in the parent's words, code or button, one line of what to do if it isn't you, stop. The captured emails that feel machine-written are the ones that add a paragraph of rationale before the action.
- For the three first-week education emails the clearest precedents are the Notion and Linear Day 1–7 runs: one topic per email, the subject is the tip, a preheader that finishes the sentence.

### Gaps
- Every named food/family app (Mealime, Paprika, Plan to Eat, Samsung Food, Yummly, eMeals, Cozi, AnyList, OurGroceries, HelloFresh) is represented here only by help-center descriptions, not by a captured email; the galleries that hold them (Really Good Emails brand pages, Milled) were 404 or bot-walled during this run, and a first-hand signup to each app would be the way to close that gap.
